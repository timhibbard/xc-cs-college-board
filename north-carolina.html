Not a ring · 40 North Carolina rows · seven groups, west to east

North Carolina

A third of this board is in one state, and until now the only way to read it as North Carolina was to scroll the Greenville table and recognise the towns. This page is the same rows regrouped — no new search, no new ring, and the Mi column still means driving miles from home — arranged the way a family would actually drive them: 40 rows in seven groups, from Brevard at 47 miles to Fayetteville State at 265. 30 are on the board (11 target, 2 deep, 16 caution and one at Verify, which here means no result to measure at all), four are cut, four sponsor cross country and no men's outdoor track, and two have no men's programme at all. Two findings are worth the page on their own. The closest schools are the thinnest: seven of them sit inside 81 miles and exactly one is a fit, while at twelve of the 30 he would arrive as the team's #1 man. And the cheapest public universities in the state are cheap by statute rather than by accident — three of the four NC Promise campuses are on this page, and the plan's non-resident rate goes up for anyone entering in 2026 or later, which the net-cost column here does not yet know about.

NC Promise, and the $2,000 a year the net-cost column does not know about

North Carolina's public system has the widest spread of non-resident tuition premiums of any state on this board — a factor of eight, from $4,000 at Western Carolina, Fayetteville State and UNC Pembroke to $32,209 at UNC Chapel Hill and $24,048 at NC State. That is not a quirk of the federal file. Those three campuses, plus Elizabeth City State, are the four NC Promise institutions: the state holds in-state tuition at $500 a semester there, and non-resident tuition at $2,500 — a difference of exactly the $4,000 a year the board computes from IPEDS, which is as clean a cross-check as this project gets.

The rate is changing, and it changes in the wrong direction for him. The UNC System publishes that non-resident students entering an NC Promise institution before 2026 pay $2,500 a semester, and that beginning with students entering in 2026 it is $3,500 — the plan's own page. The premium in the table below is therefore an entering-2025 number, and an entering-2027 freshman should add about $2,000 a year to three rows on this page: Fayetteville State (about $11,892 becomes about $13,892), UNC Pembroke ($14,260 → about $16,260) and Western Carolina ($17,315 → about $19,315). The column is left on the federal basis rather than adjusted, because every other row on this site is, and a hand-adjusted cell would be the only number here that no source states.

Even after the rise, Fayetteville State is still the cheapest target-tier school anywhere on this board — by about $74, ahead of Coastal Carolina at $13,966. That is how thin the margin is, and it is worth asking the coach about directly: a non-resident tuition waiver, of the kind athletic departments do hold, erases the whole premium and is the single largest lever on any of these three prices. The fourth NC Promise campus, Elizabeth City State, is 429.7 driving miles away and is one of the seven schools named at the bottom of this page as outside the ring.

The closest schools in the state are the thinnest, and that is the whole reading

The seven nearest North Carolina rows are all inside 81 miles — Brevard 46.6, Gardner-Webb 61.6, UNC Asheville 64.0, Warren Wilson 64.3, Montreat 72.6, Mars Hill 79.3, Western Carolina 80.5 — and one of them is a fit. At Brevard, Gardner-Webb, Mars Hill and Western Carolina he would arrive as the team's #1 man. Gardner-Webb is the sharpest version: Division 1, 62 miles, a verified CS degree, a 77% admit rate, and six finishers at the 2025 Big South championship spanning 966 seconds — the widest first-to-last championship spread of the 83 Division 1 programmes measured on this board, and still only six men, so there is no scoring seven to slot into at all. Warren Wilson, a third of a mile further out than UNC Asheville, has never finished more than six men in nine races on file.

Across the 28 measured rows on the board here, 13 have him arriving ahead of the team's fastest runner and six never finished seven men in a championship, so their slot rests on a last finisher rather than a scoring seven. Read the vs their 7th column next to the notes and never alone: being 200 seconds inside a seven that spans 400 is a measurement of a squad that has fallen apart, not of a fit. Why arriving at the front is a caution and not a win.

Exactly three programmes in the state put their #1 where this board wants him — 40 to 60 seconds ahead of his projection. They are High Point (+43s), Montreat (+49s) and NC Central (+50s), and the depth behind that front runner separates them. High Point and Montreat each finished seven men at their conference championship — he lands 62 and 85 seconds inside those scoring sevens — and each came a finisher short at the next championship, High Point's regional and Montreat's NAIA nationals. NC Central never reached seven in four races, so its +50 is measured against a sixth man. Three rows in 40, one of them Division 1 at $38,707, one NAIA in a town of 630, and one that cannot field a scoring seven: that is the honest yield of this state.

Two of the board's five published ladder disagreements are in this state, pointing opposite ways

Montreat is caution by hand where the tier ladder says target: he would be their 3rd man with their #1 49 seconds up and 85 inside their seven, which is the healthy shape — and then they took six men to the NAIA national championship, which is the warning the ladder does not read. UNC Charlotte is the mirror image — target by hand where the ladder says deep, because its two championships disagree with each other: 45 seconds outside their seven at the American Conference 8K, 25 inside at the 10K regional. Both rows are carried as disagreements rather than quietly resolved, and tools/ladder.py prints them on every run.

Region by region

Seven groups, west to east, and the membership is by campus town and by road rather than by a radius — 20 miles works in Boston because Boston is a point, and North Carolina is not. The schools sit between the state's cities: Wingate is 28 miles from Charlotte, Elon 20 from Greensboro, Campbell 35 from Raleigh, and Hickory, Salisbury and Misenheimer sit in open country belonging to neither Charlotte nor the Triad, which the Piedmont in between group is honest about instead of forcing. Each table below is one group, sorted by driving distance; the rows that are off the board are named in their group's prose and listed in full further down with a Region column. Every table here is built from the same row data as the Greenville page — that page is where sorting, filtering and the gap chart live, and nothing on this one restates a number it does not hold.

Asheville and the mountains

Six schools, 47 to 81 miles, and the group where proximity and fit pull hardest against each other. UNC Asheville is the one fit: his 3rd man, 94 seconds inside their scoring seven, a 92% admit rate, Big South, mountain terrain — with two caveats. Their #1 is only 21 seconds faster than he projects, which is tighter than the healthy band, and the $12,250 federal net price is what a North Carolinian pays; on his residency it is about $29,598, so the whole question there is a waiver. Montreat is the row to look at twice: NAIA, 762 undergraduates in a town of 630 people, and a 13.9% computer science share, second in the state only to UNC Charlotte's — his 3rd man at both championships with their #1 49 seconds up, and caution only because the second of those, the NAIA national meet, was six men deep. Warren Wilson at 64 miles is the closest row here and the thinnest on the page: nine races on file, never more than six finishers, two men at its 2025 regional, and Data Science but no computer science major. Brevard, Mars Hill and Western Carolina all put him at the front of the squad.

The High Country

Two schools over the Blue Ridge, Boone and Banner Elk, and they are opposite shapes. Appalachian State is one of only two deep rows in the state: their 7th man is 29 seconds faster than he projects across a conference 8K and a 10K regional, on a 1–7 spread averaging 63 seconds — 47 at the Sun Belt meet itself, the tightest championship seven of any row still on the board here — a real pack he would train with and not travel for in year one, at about $34,469 on his residency, with 19,444 undergraduates in a Boone of 20,032. Lees-McRae, 10 miles away and 10 miles closer to home, is the other pattern: he arrives 29 seconds ahead of their #1 on a 217-second spread, in a town of 1,473, and the federal file reports no computer science degree. The pair is grouped on its own because altitude is a real variable for a distance runner and both campuses have it.

Charlotte

Seven rows and three of the state's eleven fits — the biggest cluster of them anywhere on this page, and all three inside 116 driving miles. UNC Charlotte is the strongest row in the state on everything except the running: 15.2% of all degrees granted in computer science, the largest share here, 24,453 undergraduates, 80% admit, about $30,688 on his residency — and a target tier that is flagged rather than defended, because its 8K and its 10K disagree. Queens (Charlotte) earns the tier cleanly on running — 4th man, 46 seconds inside a seven spanning just 56 seconds, with their #1 only 11 seconds ahead of him — on the single championship they ran as a team; they sent one runner to the regional, which is not a team result and is left out. What it also reports is no computer science degree, and at 1,211 undergraduates that is worth one email to the admissions office before it is treated as a rule-out. Davidson is the highest-value application on the page: a 13% admit rate that a coach's support genuinely moves, their 4th man, and $17,379 — cheaper than nine of the twelve North Carolina public campuses on this page once the non-resident premium is applied. Behind them, Gardner-Webb (62 miles) and Belmont Abbey (90) both have him leading the squad, Johnson C. Smith finished five men, and Wingate is deep with no CS degree reported.

The Piedmont in between

Hickory, Misenheimer and Salisbury — four colleges in the farmland between Charlotte and the Triad, close to each other and in neither metro. Two are fits. Pfeiffer is the one row on the page where the college and its town are the same size: 655 undergraduates in a village of 659, a 96% admit rate, $19,076, and on the running his 5th man at both championships, 66 seconds inside their seven with their #1 38 seconds ahead. Catawba is the best fit-and-price pairing in the state that is not also a 13% admit rate: 4th-to-5th man, 105 seconds inside their seven measured twice, $17,879, a 75% admit rate and a verified CS degree — and Division 2, where partial athletic money is easiest to find. The other two are the warning that comes with a small squad: at Livingstone he would be their #1 by 136 seconds, and Lenoir-Rhyne was withdrawn from an earlier version's top picks when its scoring six at the D2 regional turned out to span 326 seconds. With 655 and 1,235 undergraduates, Pfeiffer and Catawba are also where the lab-conflict question bites hardest: ask both how a required CS course is scheduled against afternoon practice.

The Triad

Nine rows, the largest group and the most mixed: three fits, one cut, two programmes with no men's outdoor track, and the state's only row with no result at all. High Point is the answer to the question this board actually asks — their 6th man at the Big South championship and 7th at the regional, on a seven spanning 96 seconds at the conference meet, and their #1 43 seconds ahead of his projection, which is the middle of the healthy band. It is one of the most consistent reads anywhere here, and it costs $38,707. UNC Greensboro is the depth option at 157 seconds inside their seven, second in the state only to Fayetteville State's 189, with $27,316 the argument it loses; Elon averages to a 4th-to-5th-man fit at 206 miles and $41,555, the dearest of the state's eleven fits — and that average is 7th man at the CAA championship and 2nd at the regional, which is a genuine disagreement rather than a settled placing. Then the off-board half: Wake Forest is the most decisive cut on the board (137 seconds outside their scoring seven in both championships, 218 behind their #1, on a seven that spans 81 seconds — there is no soft back end to land in), Winston-Salem State and Greensboro College sponsor no men's outdoor track, and Carolina University — NAIA, 460 undergraduates — publishes a men's cross country roster and schedule and has no result on TFRRS to measure, which is what Verify means on this board: unmeasured rather than borderline.

The Sandhills and Fayetteville

The cheapest corner of the state and the emptiest. Fayetteville State is the cheapest target-tier school anywhere on this board at about $11,892 — their 4th-to-5th man on the team that won the 2025 CIAA championship, 189 seconds inside their seven, with their #1 93 seconds up — and the NC Promise rise above applies to it. Their staff directory publishes neither an email address nor a direct line for the coach, so contact there means the department's main number. UNC Pembroke is caution for the reason that is easiest to miss on a spreadsheet: at about $14,260 with a 93% admit rate it looks like the value of the state, and it won Conference Carolinas with a seven he would have led — he arrives one second ahead of their #1. Methodist finished five men at its conference meet and entered one runner at the regional, and St. Andrews, in Laurinburg, sponsors neither cross country nor track for either gender — it is listed only because a small private college with a well-known equestrian programme would otherwise look like an oversight.

The Triangle

The deepest academics in the state and the least available running. Eight rows, and six of them are off the board: Duke, UNC Chapel Hill and NC State are cut on measurement — 72, 68 and 63 seconds outside their scoring sevens, all three past the board's 45-second line — William Peace and Shaw sponsor cross country and no men's outdoor track, and Saint Augustine's has no programme, no conference and, since April 2026, a Chapter 11 filing. Two rows are live. Campbell is a fit at 244 miles: their 3rd man at the CAA championship, 103 seconds inside their seven, their #1 38 seconds ahead, an 87% admit rate and $24,516, in a Buies Creek of 3,746 people — and they took a single runner to the regional, so the fit rests on one race. NC Central is caution on a field of six — he would have been their #2 with their #1 50 seconds up, which is the right shape on a squad that could not field seven. This group is also the furthest from home in the state, and the thing that makes it thin is not the ACC: it is that the other five Raleigh and Durham programmes either dropped men's track or stopped.

Where they are

Every North Carolina row on the board, and every one off it

No radius is drawn on this map, because this page is not a search: these rows were found by a 300-mile ring centred on Greenville, South Carolina, and drawing that ring here would assert a rule the page does not use. What the shape of the pins shows instead is the state's own geography — a dense western cluster inside 81 miles, the Charlotte and Triad groups in the middle, and the Triangle strung along the eastern edge of the ring, where almost everything is cut or unavailable. The empty quarter to the east is real and it is measured at the bottom of this page.

Pins are approximate main-campus locations, nudged apart where schools share a town — Greensboro and Raleigh have four each, Charlotte and Winston-Salem three. The Mi column in every table above is real driving distance from home, returned by OSRM driving directions and rounded to the mile, so it does not correspond to how far apart two pins look.

Cut as walk-on

Four North Carolina rows are cut, all four are ACC, and three of them are in the Triangle. Not one of them is cut on reputation — every margin below is transcribed from the 2025 ACC championship and the NCAA Southeast Region: Wake Forest 137 seconds outside their scoring seven in both races, Duke 72, UNC Chapel Hill 68 and NC State 63, against a cut line of 45. NC State is the closest to that line of the three schools the widening from 250 to 300 miles added, and the interesting row is UNC Chapel Hill: its two races disagree — 96 seconds outside their seventh at the conference meet, and only 17 at a regional where their 5th through 7th ran 31:23, 31:43 and 32:29. The average decides it, and 68 is past the line. Where a cut is close, the two races are named rather than the average alone.

Seven more, and the ring stays at 300

The eastern third of the state is outside the search, and this is the measurement rather than an apology for it. Seven North Carolina colleges that sponsor men's cross country sit beyond 300 driving miles, and the nearest two miss by ten. The figures below are driving routes from the same origin and the same router that produced every Mi above — that router puts Johnson C. Smith at 100.8 and UNC Greensboro at 188.7, which is exactly what those two rows say, so these seven are comparable to the board even though nothing else about them has been read. They were re-measured when the whole Mi column was: an earlier pass had UNC Wilmington at 305, five miles out and alone at the edge, and on the router the column now uses it is 310.2 with Barton two tenths of a mile closer than it. Nothing here turns on which of the two is nearer, but the sentence that said one of them was uniquely reachable is gone, because it was an artifact of a measurement rather than a fact about the state.

What to do with this page

Four emails, in this order. High Point, because its front runner sits where this board wants him on both championships it ran, and the question there is only money — ask what academic aid stacks with a partial track offer at $38,707. Catawba and Pfeiffer, because they are fits under $20,000 at small colleges of 1,235 and 655, where the binding question is the CS timetable rather than the running: ask how a required course is scheduled against afternoon practice. And Fayetteville State, through the department's main number rather than by email, because it is the cheapest target anywhere on this board, the margin over the next one is about $74, and a non-resident tuition waiver would settle it outright. Davidson is the fifth and it is a different kind of letter: at a 13% admit rate the coach's support is the whole lever.

Two questions that are specific to this state. Ask every NC Promise coach which entering year's non-resident rate applies to a 2027 freshman, because the plan's own page says it changes with the entering class. And ask the western programmes — Brevard, Mars Hill, Warren Wilson, Montreat, Lees-McRae — how many men will be on the roster in the fall, because at every one of them the measurement says he arrives at or near the front, and being a team's #1 as a freshman is workable only if the coach is building a class around him.
