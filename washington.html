Washington DC

Eleven schools sit inside 20 miles of downtown Washington and only seven of them are available to him. Georgetown is cut on the measurement. George Washington and Washington Adventist sponsor cross country and no men's track. Maryland sponsors men's track and not men's cross country. What survives is two genuine targets — American and George Mason — at $41,943 and $42,383, which is $12,000 above the metro median. This is the metro where the board's rules cost the most.

Georgetown is cut: across the Big East championship, the Division 1 Mid-Atlantic regional and the national championship he arrives 55 seconds outside their seventh man, past the board's 45-second cut line, into a seven that spans only 97 seconds.

George Washington lists men's and women's cross country on its athletics site and no track and field of any kind, for anyone — the running would not have worked either, at 6.6 seconds outside their seventh. Washington Adventist is the same shape in the NAIA and unmeasured besides. Maryland is the reverse: men's indoor and outdoor track but no men's cross country, and the tell is on its own site, where the most recent men's cross country roster is 2012-13.

That leaves a seven-row table in a metro of five million people, which is worth knowing before planning a visit around it.

Gallaudet is the cheapest row in range, and unmeasured

$15,845 average net price, 3 miles from the centre, 4.6% computer science, and the only university in the world designed from the ground up for deaf and hard-of-hearing students. On the running there is nothing to measure: seven races on file across 2025 and 2026, never more than four finishers, and no championship in either season. Verify here means unmeasured, not borderline.

Marymount is the row to contrast it with. It also has no averageable result — two men at the 2025 Atlantic East championship and one at the Division 3 South regional, both short of the five finishers this board needs before it will average a race — but a conference championship is the meet a program brings everyone to, so a field of two there is a measurement of how thin the squad is rather than an absence of evidence. He would arrive 346 seconds ahead of their #1 at that championship. That row reads Caution, and Gallaudet, which entered no championship at all, does not.

The ones worth real effort

  1. American — D1 Patriot League, 4.5 miles out, 62% admit, and on the running he is their 5th or 6th man, 41 seconds inside their seven and 65 behind their #1. That is the healthiest measurement on this page. The problem is the price: $41,943 average net, and a 1.8% computer science share that needs a hard look at the actual department.
  2. George Mason — D1 Atlantic 10, 19.8 miles out and therefore the row most sensitive to where you put the centre pin. 14.7% CS — the strongest CS row available in this metro — 87% admit, and he lands as their 5th man, 98 seconds inside their seven. At $42,383 it is the most expensive row on the page, and it is a Virginia public, so that figure already carries the non-resident premium.
  3. Catholic — D3 Landmark, 3.2 miles, 83% admit, $29,561, and he arrives level with their #1 and 92 seconds inside their seventh. Caution tier, and the reason to keep it on the list anyway is location and price rather than depth.

Two on the money question. Howard is Division 1 in the MEAC, 1.9 miles from the centre, with a 41% admit rate — and at $50,539 it is the most expensive row on this page by $8,000, with a squad that never finished seven runners. Bowie State is the only Division 2 program in range, so it is the only place partial athletic money exists at all; it is 12.1% computer science, and he would arrive 201 seconds ahead of their #1.
