Why Pittsburgh reads differently from everywhere else in this pass

The single most common finding on this board is a program that cannot field seven men. Five of sixteen in Boston, three of fourteen in Philadelphia, four of four in Hampton Roads. In Pittsburgh it is none of the five measured programs — every one of them finished a full scoring seven in a championship — and he would lead only Point Park. Of the other two rows, one is unmeasured and one is measured thin.

That matters more than any individual row here, because depth is the thing a recruit cannot fix by arriving. A metro where the sevenths are real is a metro where the tier means what it says.

Carnegie Mellon and Duquesne, half a mile apart in effect

Carnegie Mellon is the better combination: 18.2% computer science, 3.7 miles out, $31,944, and on the running their 3rd or 4th man, 51 seconds inside a seven that he would be well into. The door is 12%. Duquesne is the accessible one: Division 1 in the Atlantic 10, 0.5 miles from the centre, 84% admit, and also their 3rd or 4th man, 52 seconds inside their seven, at $37,730 with a 4.2% CS share.

In other words the two targets split the decision cleanly: CMU is the computer science answer behind a hard door, Duquesne is the running-and-location answer behind an open one, and the two campuses are three miles apart.

What is not on this page yet

Every tier on this page comes from the published ladder run over 2025 championship results — conference, regional and national meets only. Three things the older metro pages have and this one does not, yet: no track marks, so there is no 5000m gap chart and no 1500 field here; no coach names, emails or Instagram handles; and no meet maps. The one screen that is complete is the both-sports rule — men's outdoor track sponsorship was checked school by school against the sport list each athletics site publishes itself, because TFRRS cannot answer it.

The ones worth real effort

  1. Carnegie Mellon — D3 UAA, 3.7 miles, 18.2% CS, 12% admit, $31,944, and he lands as their 3rd or 4th man, 51 seconds inside their seven and 35 behind their #1. Division 3 means no athletic money; this is an admissions and merit-aid conversation, and the CS department needs no introduction.
  2. Duquesne — D1 Atlantic 10, half a mile from downtown, 84% admit, $37,730, and the same slot: their 3rd or 4th man, 52 seconds inside their seven. Division 1 equivalency shares exist here; the CS share is 4.2%, so ask about the department directly.
  3. Pitt and Robert Morris — both deep, at 38 and 8 seconds outside their sevenths. Pitt is ACC Division 1, 3 miles out, 7.6% CS and $49,938, the most expensive row on this page and a Pennsylvania public carrying the non-resident premium. Robert Morris is Horizon League Division 1 at 17.9 miles and $23,003 — a third of Pitt's price, eight seconds off their seven, and the better value of the two by a wide margin.

Three more to know. Point Park is the metro's only Division 2 program, so it is the only partial-athletic-money door in range; it admits 97%, costs $25,942, and he would arrive 120 seconds ahead of their #1. Carlow and Chatham read the same on the table and are not the same finding. Carlow is Caution: it finished one man at the AMCC championship and two at the USCAA national meet in the same season, and eight races are on file across two seasons whose fullest fielded six — a conference championship is the race a program brings everyone to, so that is a measurement of its depth, and he would arrive 785 seconds ahead of their #1 there. Chatham is the genuinely Verify one: its entire 2025 record is one invitational with two finishers, so there is nothing to measure rather than something measured and thin.
