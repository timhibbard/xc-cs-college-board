What "target" means when nobody finished seven

The board's ladder measures him against a team's seventh man, because the seventh is the last scorer and therefore the honest test of whether he would help. Where a team never finished seven in a championship there is no seventh man, so the same thresholds run on the last finisher they did produce. Every row on this page is tiered that way.

So read Norfolk State's target tier as "he fits into the middle of what they managed to field", not "he is their fifth man". Division 1 in the MEAC, 1.9 miles from downtown, 88% admit, $26,784, 4.8% computer science, and 75 seconds behind their #1. Ask how many men are signed for the fall before ranking it; that single answer decides whether this row is real.

The other three, in order of how close they come

Hampton is the nearest thing to a squad: Division 1 in the CAA, 15 miles out, and he would arrive 20 seconds ahead of their #1 — close enough that one recruiting class changes the picture. 1.8% CS and $25,319.

Regent (D3 Coast-to-Coast, 8.5 miles, 38% admit, $19,923, 5.1% CS) and Virginia Wesleyan (D3 ODAC, 7.5 miles, $19,676) are both cheap and both thin: 107 and 168 seconds ahead of their #1 respectively.

What is not on this page yet

Two columns are filled in now: every school below carries a coach — name, title, office phone and email read off its own staff directory — and the program's Instagram, read off that school's own men's cross country page, with a degree sign where the school links only its athletics department's account. Two things the older metro pages still have and this one does not: no track marks, so there is no 5000m gap chart and no 1500 field here; and no meet maps. The one screen that is complete is the both-sports rule — men's outdoor track sponsorship was checked school by school against the sport list each athletics site publishes itself, because TFRRS cannot answer it.

The ones worth real effort

  1. Norfolk State — D1 MEAC, 1.9 miles, 88% admit, $26,784, 4.8% CS. The only target here, measured on a last finisher rather than a seventh man, 75 seconds behind their #1. Division 1 means equivalency shares exist, and an HBCU in the MEAC with an open door is a real option — but the first question is roster size, not money.
  2. Hampton — D1 CAA, 15 miles, 62% admit, $25,319. He would be their #1 by 20 seconds, which is the smallest such gap on this page by a factor of five. Caution tier today; the row most likely to look different in a year.
  3. Regent — D3 Coast-to-Coast, 8.5 miles, $19,923, and the most selective door on the page at 38%. No athletic money in Division 3, and he would lead them by nearly two minutes.

There is no Division 2 program inside 20 miles of Norfolk, so the partial athletic-aid door that exists in most metros on this board does not exist here at all: it is two Division 1 programs with equivalency shares, and two Division 3 programs with none.
