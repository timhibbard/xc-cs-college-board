## 2. Read this first: widening the radius did not help, and it overturned a recommendation

Three findings dominate this metro. Two are structural; one is a correction.

**Northwestern is out — they do not sponsor men's cross country or men's track and field at all.** ✅ Verified. Men's track & field is listed as defunct; only women's XC/track exist. **Correction: the previous version of this document was wrong about Lewis University.** It said Lewis was *"probably the single best fit in metro Chicago"* and recommended widening the radius specifically to reach it. That claim came from general knowledge of the program, not from roster data. With the radius widened to 30 miles I pulled the data, and it does not hold:

## 7. Chicago-specific considerations

**Illinois public tuition.** UIC and Chicago State are excellent value for Illinois residents and much less so from South Carolina. Confirm out-of-state rates before ranking on price.

**Winter training is a real factor.** Chicago indoor track season runs through genuine winter, and lakefront wind is not a joke. Ask coaches what indoor facility they have access to — a full 200m banked track versus a fieldhouse straightaway meaningfully affects indoor development. DePaul, Loyola, and UIC all have differing arrangements here; ask specifically.

**Training terrain.** The lakefront path is excellent for volume and flat as a table. Ask coaches where they find hills — the answer is usually Waterfall Glen, Palos, or Busse Woods, all a drive away. A program that never runs hills will limit him as a 5K/XC runner.

**The 30-mile ring is a commute, not a campus.** Wheaton, Benedictine, Lake Forest, and Purdue Northwest are all suburban or Indiana campuses. If part of the appeal of Chicago was *Chicago*, note that none of the four schools the wider radius added are in the city.
