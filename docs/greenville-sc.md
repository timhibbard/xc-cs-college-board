## 3. How to read the times

TFRRS team pages return the **single fastest athlete, in one event, in the 2026 outdoor season only**. Three consequences:

1. **It's a floor, never a ceiling** — programs whose distance runners focused on the 10K, steeple, or indoor show artificially slow outdoor 5000s.
2. **Sparse events return garbage.** The mile, 3000, and 10,000 columns are unreliable; **only the 5000 and 1500 are trustworthy.** Tell-tale contradictions in the raw data: High Point's 1500 of 3:43.9 (≈4:01 mile) vs. a "best mile" of 4:21; Lenoir-Rhyne's 3000 of 8:47 vs. a "best 1500" of 4:19; Catawba's 5000 of 14:37 vs. a "best 3000" of 10:21. Those tables were empty, not slow.
3. **No team depth is available** — one athlete per event, so I cannot see a 5th or 7th man. "Gap to their #1" is what the data supports.

## 4. The framing rule

For a ~15:15–15:25 projected 5K, **being 40–60 seconds behind a team's best runner is the healthy target.** It means training partners ahead of him and a path to scoring by junior year. Being *ahead* of a team's #1 means the program is weak, not that he's a star — distance runners develop in packs. Target: **5th to 9th man as a freshman.**

## 8. Academic and money notes

**Recruitment is an admissions lever at selective schools.** Coaches at Davidson and Emory get supported slots and will run a **pre-read** — send transcript and scores, admissions tells the coach informally whether he'd get in. This is how his running unlocks a school his stats alone wouldn't. Initiate spring of junior year.

**Where academics are an asset, not a constraint:** UNC Asheville (~90%), UNCG (~88%), ETSU (~85%), Western Carolina (~85%), and most of the D2 list. Admission is near-automatic, so all leverage shifts to **money**.

**Academic aid often beats athletic aid.** D1 XC/track is a 12.6-scholarship equivalency across a 35–45 man roster — quarter and half rides, not fulls. A strong student stacking merit aid at UNC Asheville, ETSU, High Point, Berry, or Catawba can land below the net cost of a bigger athletic offer. **Model net cost, not scholarship percentage.**

## 9. Practical notes

**CS labs collide with 3:00–5:30 practice.** Large schools (Charlotte, App State) run multiple sections; small ones (Wofford, Presbyterian, Berry, Piedmont, North Greenville, Catawba) may offer a required course in exactly one afternoon slot. **Ask every coach: "how many of your athletes major in CS or engineering, and how do you handle lab conflicts?"** This eliminates programs faster than times do.

**Contact rules.** D1 coaches may initiate contact **June 15 after sophomore year**; D2 July 15 after sophomore year; D3 essentially unrestricted. **He may email coaches any time, any age, any division** — the restriction runs one direction only.
