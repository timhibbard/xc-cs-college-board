Correction: widening to 30 miles did not deliver what I predicted

The earlier version of this page recommended widening the radius specifically to reach Lewis University, calling it "probably the single best fit in metro Chicago." With the radius widened, I pulled Lewis's actual roster data — and the recommendation does not survive it. Their team best 5000 is 13:45 (Bryan Clay Invitational), with a 29:58 10K and a 3:51 1500. That is a 95 second gap, the same band as Furman and Wingate, and by the walk-on rule applied everywhere else on this site Lewis is a cut, not a target. The claim is withdrawn.

What the extra ten miles actually added: three programs he would lead outright (Benedictine by nearly two minutes, Lake Forest by fifty seconds, Purdue Northwest by a stride) and one open question (Wheaton), now closed as caution. The wider radius did, however, put North Central in range, and cross country data has since restored it to the board — so the radius change earned exactly one school, just not the one predicted.

Second correction: Loyola Chicago is now cut too

This page ranked Loyola co-equal with DePaul on the strength of a 14:23 outdoor 5000, which read as a clean +57 second gap. They then won the Atlantic 10 championship with seven runners inside 47 seconds of each other (23:58 to 24:45), and his projection lands about 82 seconds outside that pack. The tight spread is what makes it decisive: there is no soft back end to slot into. Loyola moves to the cut list.

That is the second recommendation this page has had to withdraw, and the cause was the same both times — a single fastest 5000 is a floor, not a measure of depth. Every conclusion here that still rests on one track mark should be read as provisional.

The ones worth real effort

  1. DePaul — 3 miles from the Loop, Big East D1, a genuinely strong CS program in its School of Computing (11% of their degrees), and confirmed on cross country: their Big East seven ran 23:45 to 26:10 and he lands as their 7th man, three seconds inside it. Note why he fits — their 1–7 spread is 146 seconds, so there is a soft back end. That is a genuine opportunity to score early rather than a sign of a bad team, but it is the opposite of Loyola's pack. Best fit in the metro, and now the only one.
  2. UIC — the standing "borderline keep" question is answered, and the answer is too deep, not thin: their Missouri Valley seven ran 24:24 to 25:36 and he would be about 31 seconds outside it. It is also the cheapest strong CS option in the metro by a distance — a $10,974 average net price — so it is worth the email regardless of the tier.
  3. University of Chicago — resolved at last, and it is remarkable in two directions. Their UAA seven finished within 33 seconds of each other, the tightest pack on the entire board, and he would be about 34 seconds outside it. Meanwhile the federal data puts their admit rate at 4% and their average net price at $14,860 — the aid is extraordinary if he gets in. Athletically a stretch; academically a lottery ticket that a coach's support genuinely improves.
  4. North Central — back on the board after being cut. Their 7th man ran 25:34 at the CCIW meet and 25:46 at the D3 Midwest regional, within roughly 10 seconds of his projection. Being 8th man on a team that goes to nationals is one of the better development environments available to him; he simply would not travel in year one.
  5. Purdue Northwest — unchanged and unresolved: the only D2 program in range, therefore the only place partial athletic money exists, and he arrives level with their fastest runner. One email, asking what their incoming distance class looks like.
Wheaton College — reversed twice, and this is the settled reading

First this page called Wheaton "national-caliber" and cut it, on reputation rather than data. Then its modest outdoor table (5000 in 15:19.11) moved it to Verify, and a later pass declared that resolved in Wheaton's favor and upgraded it to Target. That upgrade was wrong, and this is the correction. Their tight CCIW pack (25:43 to 26:48) did suggest he would be their 5th man — but at the D3 Midwest regional their 4th through 7th ran 27:03 to 27:35, and he would have been their #2.

The two races only look contradictory until the course is accounted for: the CCIW course ran about 31 seconds fast at 8K, measured from four teams that raced both it and the regional. Correct for that and both results agree — he arrives near the front of this team. Wheaton is caution. Strong academics, 87% admit, and a thin training group.

The hard truth about Illinois Tech

It has one of the best CS programs on this list — 29% of its bachelor's degrees are in computer science, the highest share on the board — and it is 4 miles from downtown. But its distance squad is running 17:46 for the 5000, and the cross country result is worse: six finishers spanning 437 seconds at the D3 Midwest regional, with his projection more than two minutes ahead of their #1. That is not a training environment; it is a solo career. If CS leads the decision and he accepts that, fine — but go in knowing it, and ask the coach directly what they are recruiting.

Three things specific to Chicago

Illinois public tuition — and it is less of a problem than expected. UIC and Chicago State charge out-of-state rates to a South Carolina applicant, but UIC's average net price is still $10,974, the lowest of any school in this metro and lower than most of the D3 privates. Confirm the out-of-state figure directly, since a federal average blends in-state students, but do not rule it out on price.

Winter training is a real factor. The indoor season runs through genuine Chicago winter, and lakefront wind is not a joke. Ask what indoor facility each program has access to — a full 200m banked track versus a fieldhouse straightaway meaningfully changes indoor development. DePaul, UIC, and North Central all have different arrangements; ask specifically.

Training terrain. The lakefront path is excellent for volume and flat as a table. Ask where they find hills; the answer is usually Waterfall Glen, Palos, or Busse Woods, all a drive away. A program that never runs hills will limit him as a 5K and cross country runner.
