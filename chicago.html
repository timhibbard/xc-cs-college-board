Correction: widening to 30 miles did not deliver what I predicted

The earlier version of this page recommended widening the radius specifically to reach Lewis University, calling it "probably the single best fit in metro Chicago." With the radius widened, I pulled Lewis's actual roster data — and the recommendation does not survive it. Their team best 5000 is 13:45 (Bryan Clay Invitational), with a 29:58 10K and a 3:51 1500. That is a 95 second gap, the same band as Furman and Wingate, and by the walk-on rule applied everywhere else on this site Lewis is a cut, not a target. The claim is withdrawn.

What the extra ten miles actually added: three programs he would lead outright (Benedictine by nearly two minutes, Lake Forest by fifty seconds, Purdue Northwest by a stride) and one genuine open question (Wheaton). DePaul and Loyola remain the only real fits in Chicago — that conclusion is now better supported, not weaker.

The ones worth real effort

  1. DePaul — 3 miles from the Loop, Big East D1, a genuinely strong CS program in its School of Computing, and a clean 58 second gap to their #1. An 8:23 3000 and a 29:52 10K confirm a functioning distance squad rather than one fast athlete. Best fit in the metro, comfortably.
  2. Loyola Chicago — 8 miles, Atlantic 10, ~80% acceptance, a 57 second gap, lakefront campus with real training access. Essentially co-equal with DePaul; choose on CS department fit and money.
  3. UIC — the cheapest strong option with a good CS department, but resolve the depth question first. Their 13:56 best and 28:56 10K are a strong top end; one fast senior is not depth.
  4. University of Chicago — only if his academic profile supports a ~5% admit rate. Elite CS, good conference (the same UAA as NYU and Emory). Still a complete blank on times after repeated attempts to resolve its TFRRS page, and that is the single highest-value lookup left in this metro.
  5. Wheaton College — the one item the wider radius genuinely opened up, and it is a question rather than an answer. See below.
Wheaton College is the open question, and I got it wrong once already

I had Wheaton in the cut list described as a "national-caliber D3 program." Its outdoor table does not support that: 1500 in 4:08.73, 3000 in 9:12.40, 5000 in 15:19.11, 10,000 in 31:52.17. Those four marks are internally consistent with each other and modest — this is not the sparse-table artifact that produced the Clemson error, because here every event agrees. The "national-caliber" claim is withdrawn.

But 15:19 is one second off his own projection, which would make him their #1 as a freshman — and that is hard to square with Wheaton's actual cross country reputation in the CCIW. The most likely explanation is that their distance men skipped outdoor track. It sits in Verify rather than either tier until 2025 cross country results settle it. Worth the email either way: 25 miles, strong academics, and a real chance the running is better than the table says.

The hard truth about Illinois Tech

It has one of the best CS programs on this list and it is 4 miles from downtown — but its distance squad is running 17:46 for the 5000. He would beat their best runner by two and a half minutes. That is not a training environment; it is a solo career. If CS leads the decision and he accepts that, fine — but go in knowing it, and ask the coach directly what they are recruiting.

Three things specific to Chicago

Illinois public tuition. UIC and Chicago State are excellent value for Illinois residents and much less so from South Carolina. Confirm out-of-state rates before ranking either on price.

Winter training is a real factor. The indoor season runs through genuine Chicago winter, and lakefront wind is not a joke. Ask what indoor facility each program has access to — a full 200m banked track versus a fieldhouse straightaway meaningfully changes indoor development. DePaul, Loyola, and UIC all have different arrangements; ask specifically.

Training terrain. The lakefront path is excellent for volume and flat as a table. Ask where they find hills; the answer is usually Waterfall Glen, Palos, or Busse Woods, all a drive away. A program that never runs hills will limit him as a 5K and cross country runner.
