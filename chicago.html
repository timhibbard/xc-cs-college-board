Chicago

This is by far the smallest of the three boards, and the Division 1 rule is why it is now very small indeed: three schools. Metro Chicago is a Division 3 city for men's distance running. Of the eleven measured programs inside 20 miles, ten archived rows are D3, D2 or NAIA, and the three that remain are DePaul, UIC and Chicago State. DePaul is still the only target-tier school in the metro, and that has now been true through four versions of this page, two different ranking methods, and a sweep that measured every school here. Nothing has been thrown away — every archived school keeps its measurement, its coach and its own page, in the table at the bottom — and reading that table is the fastest way to see what the rule actually cost here, which is one Deep-tier school (the University of Chicago) and both of the metro's scholarship doors.

What the 20-mile radius costs, stated plainly

Five schools leave the board: North Central (28 mi), Purdue Northwest (25), Wheaton College (25), Benedictine (25) and Lake Forest (30). Lewis University (30) leaves the cut list. Two of those losses matter and three do not.

North Central is the real loss. A perennial D3 national-championship program whose 7th man ran 25:34 at the CCIW meet and 25:46 at the D3 Midwest regional — roughly five seconds outside his projection once the CCIW course is corrected. It was the best training environment on the Chicago list and one of only three Deep-tier schools here. Purdue Northwest is the other loss, not on merit but on mechanism: it was the only Division 2 program in range, so it was the only Chicago school where partial athletic money existed at all. Roosevelt University now fills that slot — D2, GLIAC, one mile from the centre of the Loop — and it is a straight upgrade on location if nothing else.

The three that do not matter were all caution tier: he would have led Benedictine by nearly two minutes and Lake Forest by fifty seconds. Wheaton was the interesting one and it had already settled at caution — its tight CCIW pack (25:43–26:48) suggested he would be their 5th man, but at the D3 Midwest regional their 4th through 7th ran 27:03–27:35 and he would have been their #2; correcting the CCIW course, which ran about 31 seconds fast, the two races agree.

The Division 1 rule has since made this whole callout academic. All six of those schools are Division 3 or Division 2 — North Central, Wheaton, Benedictine and Lake Forest are D3, Purdue Northwest and Lewis are D2 — so every one of them would be in the archive at 20 miles or at 30. It is worth keeping anyway, because North Central at 28 miles remains the best training environment this project has found in Chicagoland, and if the divisional rule ever comes off it is the first name to revisit.

The Lewis University recommendation, now reversed twice over

An earlier version of this page called Lewis University "probably the single best fit in metro Chicago" and recommended widening the radius specifically to reach it. Its roster data then killed it: a team best 5000 of 13:45, a 29:58 10K and a 3:51 1500 — a 95 second gap, the same band as Furman and Wingate. At 30 miles it sat on the cut list as a documented reversal. At 20 miles it is out of range as well as out of contention, and it is Division 2 besides, so it no longer appears anywhere on this board.

Worth keeping the lesson rather than the school: the original error was reading one fast 5000 as a measure of a squad's depth. It happened here with Lewis and again with Loyola, and it is why this page now ranks on cross country results.

Loyola Chicago is cut, reversing this page's other recommendation

This page once ranked Loyola co-equal with DePaul on the strength of a 14:23 outdoor 5000, which read as a clean +57 second gap. They then won the Atlantic 10 championship with seven runners inside 47 seconds of each other (23:58 to 24:45), and his projection lands about 75 seconds outside that pack. The tight spread is what makes it decisive: there is no soft back end to slot into.

Two Chicago schools have no program to join

Trinity Christian is the second, for a different reason: it is closing. It is NAIA, so its row now sits in the Division 1 archive below rather than here, and that row says "closing" rather than a tier.

The ones worth real effort

  1. DePaul — 3 miles from the Loop, Big East D1, a genuinely strong CS program in its School of Computing (11% of their degrees), and confirmed on cross country: their Big East seven ran 23:45 to 26:10 and he lands as their 7th man, ten seconds inside it. Note why he fits — their 1–7 spread is 146 seconds, so there is a soft back end. That is a genuine opportunity to score early rather than a sign of a bad team, but it is the opposite of Loyola's pack. Best fit in the metro, and now the only one.
  2. UIC — the standing "borderline keep" question is answered, and the answer is too deep, not thin: their Missouri Valley seven ran 24:24 to 25:36 and he would be about 24 seconds outside it. It used to be the cheapest strong CS option in the metro by a distance on its $10,974 average net price — but that federal average covers Illinois residents only, and on his residency it is about $26,520 — still the middle of a three-school ladder rather than the bargain it looked like. It is worth the email regardless of the tier, with a non-resident waiver question attached.
  3. Chicago State — the third and last Division 1 program, 10 miles out, and it is at caution for the plainest possible reason: five finishers at the NEC championship spanning 514 seconds, and he would arrive more than a minute ahead of their #1. Five is exactly enough to score and no more. Its one genuine advantage is money — it charges one flat $12,335 rate to residents and non-residents alike, which is unusual enough to be worth an email even at this tier. Ask how many men are signed for the fall before ranking it.

Two names that used to head this list are now archived. The University of Chicago is seven miles out and remarkable in two directions — their UAA seven finished within 33 seconds of each other, the tightest pack in this project, with his projection about 27 seconds off the back of it, against a 4% admit rate and a $14,860 average net price. It is Division 3, so it is the one school the divisional rule genuinely cost this metro. Roosevelt was the metro's only scholarship program, a mile from the centre of the Loop and transitioning into Division 2 — and at the 2025 GLIAC championship four runners finished, spanning 377 seconds, below the five a team needs to score. That one was already dead on measurement before the rule reached it.

Calumet College came off before either of them. It was the last unmeasured school in the metro — NAIA, 20 miles out, a thin-looking CS program — and the reason it stayed unmeasured turned out to be the answer: it sponsors men's cross country and no men's outdoor track, so it cannot give him both seasons. See below.

The hard truth about Illinois Tech — which is the hard truth about this metro

Illinois Tech is archived now, and it is the single best illustration of why. It has one of the best CS programs anywhere in this project — 29% of its bachelor's degrees are in computer science — and it is 4 miles from downtown. But its distance squad is running 17:46 for the 5000, and the cross country result is worse: six finishers spanning 437 seconds at the D3 Midwest regional, with his projection more than two minutes ahead of their #1. That is not a training environment; it is a solo career. If CS alone leads the decision and he accepts that, it is still a real option — but go in knowing it, and ask the coach directly what they are recruiting.

And Illinois Tech is not unusual here. Across all eleven measured programs in range, five never finished seven runners — Illinois Tech, Elmhurst, Dominican, Chicago State and Roosevelt — and every school in the metro outside DePaul, UIC and the University of Chicago puts him at their #1. On the Division 1 board that reduces to one program short of seven, Chicago State at five finishers, and one program he would lead, the same one. Chicago's problem was never the radius and it is not really the divisional rule either: beyond three good programs there is very little men's distance running inside the city, and two of those three are D1.

The Division 1 rule closed both of this metro's scholarship doors, and it is worth being explicit about which. Roosevelt was the only Division 2 program inside 20 miles, so it was the only place partial athletic aid existed — and it finished four runners at its conference meet, so it was gone on measurement first. The 1500 in May

Every school page here now carries its 2026 conference-championship 1500 field with his projected 4:01 dropped into it on time, plus that program's own 1500 depth chart for the season. On the all-divisions board Chicago was the one metro where the 1500 made things look better; under the Division 1 rule that reverses, and the reversal is the honest reading.

He would have made all three conference finals and scored in one. Across the wider eleven-school sweep he would have made every final and scored — top eight — at eight of them, the highest hit rate of the three metros. But look at which eight: Roosevelt, Saint Xavier, Chicago State, Concordia, Dominican, Elmhurst, Illinois Tech and North Park. Those are the CCAC, NACC, GLIAC and CCIW fields, and scoring in them is not the same achievement as scoring in the Big East. On this board the only scoring finish left is Chicago State's NEC, where the twelfth man ran 4:13.1. In the two conferences that actually matter here he would have been 12th of 13 in the Big East final and 10th in the Missouri Valley — in the race, at the back of it.

And the Division 1 rule removes the thinness entirely. At six of the eleven programs in range he would have been their fastest 1500 man — Roosevelt, Concordia, Dominican, Elmhurst, Illinois Tech and North Park, all archived. At none of the three schools on this board would he be their #1, and the median team best is 3:48.4 against his 4:01. DePaul is the healthy answer — their #4 of nine men on the season, and their #1 went 3:43.8, which is a runner to chase for four years; UIC has him #5 of nine and Chicago State #3 of five. All three took a 1500 runner past the conference meet into a postseason round, which is not true of any archived program here. How the 1500 comparison is built.

Three things specific to Chicago

Illinois public tuition — and it is more of a problem than this page used to say. UIC and Chicago State are the only two publics here, and both would charge a South Carolina applicant out-of-state rates. UIC's $10,974 average net price looked like the lowest in the metro, but the federal net price at a public is computed from in-state students only; Illinois charges non-residents $15,546 more in tuition, so his planning figure is about $26,520 (how that is calculated). Chicago State is the exception: it charges one flat rate to everyone, so its $12,335 stands as reported — one of only two publics on the whole board where that is true, and it makes Chicago State the cheapest school on this board by $14,000. In the wider sweep two privates undercut both, Saint Xavier at $10,970 and Dominican at $11,745, and both come back Caution on the running; both are archived. With only three schools left the median here is simply UIC's $26,520.

Winter training is a real factor. The indoor season runs through genuine Chicago winter, and lakefront wind is not a joke. Ask what indoor facility each program has access to — a full 200m banked track versus a fieldhouse straightaway meaningfully changes indoor development. DePaul, UIC and Chicago State all have different arrangements; ask specifically. Each school's page maps every indoor meet it travelled to last year, which is a decent proxy for how seriously a program treats the season.

Training terrain, and now with less of it. The lakefront path is excellent for volume and flat as a table. Ask where they find hills; the answer is usually Waterfall Glen, Palos or Busse Woods — all a drive away, and at 20 miles every school on this list is now inside the flat part of Chicagoland. A program that never runs hills will limit him as a 5K and cross country runner.
