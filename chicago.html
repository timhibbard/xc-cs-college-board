This is the smallest of the three boards and the radius change made it smaller still. At 20 miles it is a genuinely urban list — every school on it is in the city or on its immediate edge — but it costs five schools, including the two that were doing the most work here. What the 20-mile radius costs, stated plainly

Five schools leave the board: North Central (28 mi), Purdue Northwest (25), Wheaton College (25), Benedictine (25) and Lake Forest (30). Lewis University (30) leaves the cut list. Two of those losses matter and three do not.

North Central is the real loss. A perennial D3 national-championship program whose 7th man ran 25:34 at the CCIW meet and 25:46 at the D3 Midwest regional — roughly ten seconds outside his projection. It was the best training environment on the Chicago list and one of only three Deep-tier schools here. Purdue Northwest is the other loss, not on merit but on mechanism: it was the only Division 2 program in range, so it was the only Chicago school where partial athletic money existed at all. Roosevelt University now fills that slot — D2, GLIAC, one mile from the centre of the Loop — and it is a straight upgrade on location if nothing else.

The three that do not matter were all caution tier: he would have led Benedictine by nearly two minutes and Lake Forest by fifty seconds. Wheaton was the interesting one and it had already settled at caution — its tight CCIW pack (25:43–26:48) suggested he would be their 5th man, but at the D3 Midwest regional their 4th through 7th ran 27:03–27:35 and he would have been their #2; correcting the CCIW course, which ran about 31 seconds fast, the two races agree.

The Lewis University recommendation, now reversed twice over

An earlier version of this page called Lewis University "probably the single best fit in metro Chicago" and recommended widening the radius specifically to reach it. Its roster data then killed it: a team best 5000 of 13:45, a 29:58 10K and a 3:51 1500 — a 95 second gap, the same band as Furman and Wingate. At 30 miles it sat on the cut list as a documented reversal. At 20 miles it is out of range as well as out of contention, so it no longer appears anywhere on this board.

Worth keeping the lesson rather than the school: the original error was reading one fast 5000 as a measure of a squad's depth. It happened here with Lewis and again with Loyola, and it is why this page now ranks on cross country results.

Loyola Chicago is cut, reversing this page's other recommendation

This page once ranked Loyola co-equal with DePaul on the strength of a 14:23 outdoor 5000, which read as a clean +57 second gap. They then won the Atlantic 10 championship with seven runners inside 47 seconds of each other (23:58 to 24:45), and his projection lands about 82 seconds outside that pack. The tight spread is what makes it decisive: there is no soft back end to slot into.

The ones worth real effort

  1. DePaul — 3 miles from the Loop, Big East D1, a genuinely strong CS program in its School of Computing (11% of their degrees), and confirmed on cross country: their Big East seven ran 23:45 to 26:10 and he lands as their 7th man, three seconds inside it. Note why he fits — their 1–7 spread is 146 seconds, so there is a soft back end. That is a genuine opportunity to score early rather than a sign of a bad team, but it is the opposite of Loyola's pack. Best fit in the metro, and now the only one.
  2. UIC — the standing "borderline keep" question is answered, and the answer is too deep, not thin: their Missouri Valley seven ran 24:24 to 25:36 and he would be about 31 seconds outside it. It is also the cheapest strong CS option in the metro by a distance — a $10,974 average net price — so it is worth the email regardless of the tier.
  3. University of Chicago — resolved at last, and it is remarkable in two directions. Their UAA seven finished within 33 seconds of each other, the tightest pack on the entire board, and he would be about 34 seconds outside it. Meanwhile the federal data puts their admit rate at 4% and their average net price at $14,860 — the aid is extraordinary if he gets in. Athletically a stretch; academically a lottery ticket that a coach's support genuinely improves.
  4. Roosevelt — measured, and it is bad news. It is still the only scholarship program left in metro Chicago now that Purdue Northwest is out of range, still a mile from the centre of the Loop, and still transitioning into Division 2. But at the 2025 GLIAC championship four runners finished, spanning 377 seconds, and a team needs five to score. He would have been their #1 as a freshman with nobody within six minutes. That is a roster question, not a recruiting one: ask how many men are signed for the fall before spending any more thought on it.
  5. Calumet College — the one school in this metro still unmeasured, NAIA, 20 miles out, and the CS program looks thin rather than verified. Low priority, but it is the only lookup left here.
The hard truth about Illinois Tech

It has one of the best CS programs on this list — 29% of its bachelor's degrees are in computer science, the highest share on the board — and it is 4 miles from downtown. But its distance squad is running 17:46 for the 5000, and the cross country result is worse: six finishers spanning 437 seconds at the D3 Midwest regional, with his projection more than two minutes ahead of their #1. That is not a training environment; it is a solo career. If CS leads the decision and he accepts that, fine — but go in knowing it, and ask the coach directly what they are recruiting.

And Illinois Tech is not unusual here. Now that all eleven programs in range have a result, five of them never finished seven runners — Illinois Tech, Elmhurst, Dominican, Chicago State and Roosevelt — and every school in the metro outside DePaul, UIC and Chicago puts him at their #1. Chicago's problem was never the radius. It is that beyond three good programs there is very little men's distance running inside the city.

Three things specific to Chicago

Illinois public tuition — and it is less of a problem than expected. UIC and Chicago State charge out-of-state rates to a South Carolina applicant, but UIC's average net price is still $10,974, the lowest of any school in this metro and lower than most of the D3 privates. Confirm the out-of-state figure directly, since a federal average blends in-state students, but do not rule it out on price.

Winter training is a real factor. The indoor season runs through genuine Chicago winter, and lakefront wind is not a joke. Ask what indoor facility each program has access to — a full 200m banked track versus a fieldhouse straightaway meaningfully changes indoor development. DePaul, UIC and Illinois Tech all have different arrangements; ask specifically. Each school's page maps every indoor meet it travelled to last year, which is a decent proxy for how seriously a program treats the season.

Training terrain, and now with less of it. The lakefront path is excellent for volume and flat as a table. Ask where they find hills; the answer is usually Waterfall Glen, Palos or Busse Woods — all a drive away, and at 20 miles every school on this list is now inside the flat part of Chicagoland. A program that never runs hills will limit him as a 5K and cross country runner.
