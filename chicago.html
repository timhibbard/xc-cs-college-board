Chicago

This is by far the smallest of the three boards: eleven schools, every one of them measured. Metro Chicago is a Division 3 city for men's distance running — six of the eleven are D3, three are D1, one is D2 and one is NAIA — which is the single most important fact about recruiting here, because D3 cannot offer athletic money at all. DePaul is the only target-tier school in the metro, and that has now been true through five versions of this page, two different ranking methods, a 30-mile ring and a 20-mile one. A Division-1-only rule was tried on this board and reversed; in this metro it cut the list to three and cost one Deep-tier school, the University of Chicago, plus both of the places where athletic money exists at all.

What the 20-mile radius costs, stated plainly

Five schools leave the board: North Central (28 mi), Purdue Northwest (25), Wheaton College (25), Benedictine (25) and Lake Forest (30). Lewis University (30) leaves the cut list. Two of those losses matter and three do not.

North Central is the real loss. A perennial D3 national-championship program whose 7th man ran 25:34 at the CCIW meet and 25:46 at the D3 Midwest regional — roughly five seconds outside his projection once the CCIW course is corrected. It was the best training environment on the Chicago list and one of only three Deep-tier schools here. Purdue Northwest is the other loss, not on merit but on mechanism: it was the only Division 2 program in range, so it was the only Chicago school where partial athletic money existed at all. Roosevelt University now fills that slot — D2, GLIAC, one mile from the centre of the Loop — and it is a straight upgrade on location if nothing else.

The three that do not matter were all caution tier: he would have led Benedictine by nearly two minutes and Lake Forest by fifty seconds. Wheaton was the interesting one and it had already settled at caution — its tight CCIW pack (25:43–26:48) suggested he would be their 5th man, but at the D3 Midwest regional their 4th through 7th ran 27:03–27:35 and he would have been their #2; correcting the CCIW course, which ran about 31 seconds fast, the two races agree.

North Central at 28 miles remains the best training environment this project has found in Chicagoland, and it is the first name to revisit if the radius ever moves again. That is a live question rather than a closed one: this board is no longer Division 1 only, so a D3 program on the wrong side of the ring is excluded by geography alone.

The Lewis University recommendation, now reversed twice over

An earlier version of this page called Lewis University "probably the single best fit in metro Chicago" and recommended widening the radius specifically to reach it. Its roster data then killed it: a team best 5000 of 13:45, a 29:58 10K and a 3:51 1500 — a 95 second gap, the same band as Furman and Wingate. At 30 miles it sat on the cut list as a documented reversal. At 20 miles it is out of range as well as out of contention, so it no longer appears anywhere on this board.

Worth keeping the lesson rather than the school: the original error was reading one fast 5000 as a measure of a squad's depth. It happened here with Lewis and again with Loyola, and it is why this page now ranks on cross country results.

Loyola Chicago is cut, reversing this page's other recommendation

This page once ranked Loyola co-equal with DePaul on the strength of a 14:23 outdoor 5000, which read as a clean +57 second gap. They then won the Atlantic 10 championship with seven runners inside 47 seconds of each other (23:58 to 24:45), and his projection lands about 75 seconds outside that pack. The tight spread is what makes it decisive: there is no soft back end to slot into.

The ones worth real effort

  1. DePaul — 3 miles from the Loop, Big East D1, a genuinely strong CS program in its School of Computing (11% of their degrees), and confirmed on cross country: their Big East seven ran 23:45 to 26:10 and he lands as their 7th man, ten seconds inside it. Note why he fits — their 1–7 spread is 146 seconds, so there is a soft back end. That is a genuine opportunity to score early rather than a sign of a bad team, but it is the opposite of Loyola's pack. Best fit in the metro, and the only target-tier one.
  2. UIC — the standing "borderline keep" question is answered, and the answer is too deep, not thin: their Missouri Valley seven ran 24:24 to 25:36 and he would be about 24 seconds outside it. It used to be the cheapest strong CS option in the metro by a distance on its $10,974 average net price — but that federal average covers Illinois residents only, and on his residency it is about $26,520 — still the middle of a three-school ladder rather than the bargain it looked like. It is worth the email regardless of the tier, with a non-resident waiver question attached.
  3. Chicago State — the third Division 1 program, 10 miles out, and it is at caution for the plainest possible reason: five finishers at the NEC championship spanning 514 seconds, and he would arrive more than a minute ahead of their #1. Five is exactly enough to score and no more. Its one genuine advantage is money — it charges one flat $12,335 rate to residents and non-residents alike, which is unusual enough to be worth an email even at this tier. Ask how many men are signed for the fall before ranking it.

Two more names belong on this list with an asterisk. The University of Chicago is seven miles out and remarkable in two directions — their UAA seven finished within 33 seconds of each other, the tightest pack in this project, with his projection about 27 seconds off the back of it, against a 4% admit rate and a $14,860 average net price. It sits at deep rather than target, and being Division 3 it can offer no athletic money at all, so the whole conversation there is admissions and merit aid. Roosevelt is the metro's only Division 2 program, a mile from the centre of the Loop, so it is the only place partial athletic aid exists — and at the 2025 GLIAC championship four runners finished, spanning 377 seconds, below the five a team needs to score. The money is real and the team may not be; ask how many men are signed.

Calumet College came off before either of them. It was the last unmeasured school in the metro — NAIA, 20 miles out, a thin-looking CS program — and the reason it stayed unmeasured turned out to be the answer: it sponsors men's cross country and no men's outdoor track, so it cannot give him both seasons. See below.

The hard truth about Illinois Tech — which is the hard truth about this metro

Illinois Tech is the single best illustration of what this metro is. It has one of the best CS programs anywhere in this project — 29% of its bachelor's degrees are in computer science — and it is 4 miles from downtown. But its distance squad is running 17:46 for the 5000, and the cross country result is worse: six finishers spanning 437 seconds at the D3 Midwest regional, with his projection more than two minutes ahead of their #1. That is not a training environment; it is a solo career. If CS alone leads the decision and he accepts that, it is still a real option — but go in knowing it, and ask the coach directly what they are recruiting.

And Illinois Tech is not unusual here. Across all eleven measured programs in range, five never finished seven runners — Illinois Tech, Elmhurst, Dominican, Chicago State and Roosevelt — and every school in the metro outside DePaul, UIC and the University of Chicago puts him at their #1 — eight of eleven. The Division-1-only rule hid this rather than fixing it: on that board only Chicago State was short of seven and only Chicago State would he lead, which reads like a healthy metro and is really a sample of three. Chicago's problem was never the radius and it is not the divisional rule either: beyond three good programs there is very little men's distance running inside the city.

There are exactly two athletic-money doors in this metro outside Division 1, and both come with a warning. Roosevelt is the only Division 2 program inside 20 miles, so it is the only place partial athletic aid exists at all — and it finished four runners at its conference meet. NAIA allows 12 scholarships for men's XC and track and has looser eligibility rules than the NCAA; that door is Saint Xavier alone at $10,970, since Trinity Christian is closing and Calumet College sponsors no men's track, and he would arrive as Saint Xavier's #1. The other six schools are Division 3, where athletic money does not exist by rule and the levers are admissions and merit aid. On the D1 ladder the money is equivalency shares and academic aid: Chicago State at a flat $12,335 to everyone, UIC at about $26,520 on his residency (the $10,974 average net price is an Illinois-resident figure) and DePaul at $30,902.

The 1500 in May

Every school page here now carries its 2026 conference-championship 1500 field with his projected 4:01 dropped into it on time, plus that program's own 1500 depth chart for the season. Chicago is the one metro where the 1500 makes things look better than cross country does, and that is a warning about the 1500 rather than good news about Chicago.

His 4:01 clears the last qualifying time in all eleven of these conference finals, and scores — top eight — in eight of them, the highest hit rate of the three metros. One caveat on the denominator first: Concordia Chicago entered nobody in the 1500 at the CCIW meet, so its row is a comparison against a field its own team did not contest. Then look at which eight: Roosevelt, Saint Xavier, Chicago State, Concordia, Dominican, Elmhurst, Illinois Tech and North Park. Those are the CCAC, NACC, GLIAC and CCIW fields, and scoring in them is not the same achievement as scoring in the Big East. In the two conferences with the strongest programs here he would have been 12th of 13 in the Big East final and 10th in the Missouri Valley — in the race, at the back of it, which is the healthy version.

And the thinness is right there in the same numbers. At six of the eleven programs in range he would be their fastest 1500 man — Roosevelt, Concordia, Dominican, Elmhurst, Illinois Tech and North Park — and the metro's median team best is 4:09.5 against his 4:01. Narrow to Division 1 and that median becomes 3:48.4 and the count of programs he would lead becomes zero, which is the same three schools looking better because the other eight are gone. DePaul is the healthy answer — their #4 of nine men on the season, and their #1 went 3:43.8, which is a runner to chase for four years; UIC has him #5 of nine, the University of Chicago #6 of seventeen, and Chicago State #3 of five. Only five programs in this metro have a season 1500 leader faster than 4:01 — those four plus Saint Xavier at 3:58.8 — and none of the eleven took a 1500 runner past its conference meet into a postseason round, which is the plainest single measure of the level here. Three things specific to Chicago

Illinois public tuition — and it is more of a problem than this page used to say. UIC and Chicago State are the only two publics here, and both would charge a South Carolina applicant out-of-state rates. UIC's $10,974 average net price looked like the lowest in the metro, but the federal net price at a public is computed from in-state students only; Illinois charges non-residents $15,546 more in tuition, so his planning figure is about $26,520 (how that is calculated). Chicago State is the exception: it charges one flat rate to everyone, so its $12,335 stands as reported — one of only two publics on the whole board where that is true, and it makes Chicago State the cheapest of the three Division 1 options by $14,000. Two privates undercut it outright — Saint Xavier at $10,970 and Dominican at $11,745 — and both come back Caution on the running, which is the trade this metro keeps offering. The metro's median net price on his residency is $18,425.

Winter training is a real factor. The indoor season runs through genuine Chicago winter, and lakefront wind is not a joke. Ask what indoor facility each program has access to — a full 200m banked track versus a fieldhouse straightaway meaningfully changes indoor development. DePaul, UIC, Chicago State and the D3 colleges all have very different arrangements; ask specifically. Each school's page maps every indoor meet it travelled to last year, which is a decent proxy for how seriously a program treats the season.

Training terrain, and now with less of it. The lakefront path is excellent for volume and flat as a table. Ask where they find hills; the answer is usually Waterfall Glen, Palos or Busse Woods — all a drive away, and at 20 miles every school on this list is now inside the flat part of Chicagoland. A program that never runs hills will limit him as a 5K and cross country runner.
