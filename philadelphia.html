La Salle is the most unusual row in this pass

A Division 1 program in the Atlantic 10 that admits 97% of applicants, 6.9 miles from City Hall, at an average net price of $19,409 — and on the running it is a genuine fit rather than a consolation: across the A10 championship and the Division 1 Mid-Atlantic regional he lands as their 7th man, nine seconds inside their scoring seven, 79 seconds behind their #1. That combination — Division 1, open admissions, sub-$20,000 and a real slot — does not appear anywhere else on this board.

Two things to check before ranking it. Its computer science share is 3.5% of degrees, which is thin, so ask what the actual course catalogue and faculty look like; and it reports no SAT range at all, which usually means test-optional admissions with a small reporting sample rather than anything sinister.

Villanova and Temple, the two obvious names, are both unavailable

Villanova is cut, and not narrowly: their championship seven spans 68 seconds and he arrives 73 seconds outside the back of it, 141 behind their #1. A program that has won national titles in this event; there is no soft back end to slot into.

Temple is the more frustrating row. On the running it is a target — their 6th or 7th man, 64 seconds behind their #1 and 31 inside their seventh across the American championship and the Division 1 regional — but its athletics site lists men's cross country, women's cross country and women's track and field. Men's track and field is gone, which matches the sports Temple cut in 2013. It is on the no-track list below rather than in the table, and it is the first row to reopen if cross country alone ever becomes acceptable.

The ones worth real effort

  1. La Salle — D1 Atlantic 10, 6.9 mi, 97% admit, $19,409, and he is their 7th man, 9 seconds inside the seven. The single most actionable row in this pass. Division 1 means equivalency-share athletic money exists; ask what a partial share looks like and what the CS department actually offers.
  2. Penn — 1.9 miles from City Hall, Ivy League, 7.3% CS, $28,699 on need-based aid, 5% admit. He is their 5th man, seven seconds inside their seven — a stronger slot than La Salle's, behind a much harder door. No athletic scholarships in the Ivy League.
  3. Haverford and Swarthmore — the Centennial Conference pair, 10.4 and 16.3 miles out, 9.5% and 11.4% CS, $25,314 and $23,149, admitting 12% and 7%. Haverford is the better running fit of the two: their 4th man, 45 seconds inside their seven. Both are Division 3, so admissions and merit aid are the only levers.

Three more with an asterisk. Saint Joseph's is the closest call on the entire board — one second outside their seventh man, which is deep by a rounding error, at a 89% admit rate and $29,689. Penn State Abington has the highest CS share on the page at 17.6% and admits 97%, but he would arrive 13 seconds ahead of their #1. And Thomas Jefferson and Rosemont sit at Verify meaning unmeasured, not borderline: Jefferson's only 2025 championship was the Division 2 national meet with a single individual qualifier, and Rosemont's most recent men's cross country roster is 2024 with no 2025 schedule and no appearance at its conference championship. Confirm Rosemont still fields a team before anything else.
