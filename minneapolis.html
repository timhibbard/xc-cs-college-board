Minneapolis

Nine schools, three divisions and five conferences — Big Ten, Summit League, Northern Sun, MIAC and UMAC — and every one of them is inside eleven miles of road. No other ring on this board is that compact: Boston and New York hold more programs at that range but keep spreading past it, and here the whole board is the width of one commute. The running is the usual story told faster. One school is a fit — St Thomas, 5.5 miles out, where he would be 18 seconds outside a scoring seven. One is too deep — Minnesota. At six of the remaining seven he would arrive as the fastest man on the roster, though the two closest of those, Bethel and Macalester, miss by only 11 and 19 seconds averaged across their championships — one recruit either way would flip them, and they are still on the wrong side today.

One fit, one too deep, and nothing else within two years of either

St Thomas is the row this metro rests on. Their Summit League seven ran 24:23–25:18 and he lands 18 seconds outside it, 73 behind their #1 — and fall 2026 says the same thing from three more races, 77 and 14. That is the gap this board is looking for: a group he would train at the back of rather than lead. 4.3% of its degrees are computer science, it admits 85%, and at $29,155 it is the second most expensive row here. It moved up from Division 3 to Division 1 in 2021, which is worth a direct question to the coach: what the distance group is being built toward, and what scholarship money actually exists in it.

Minnesota is the near miss, and it is nearer than deep sounds. Their Big Ten seven ran 24:04–24:51 — a 47-second pack — and he would be their 8th man, 45 seconds outside; over 10K at the Midwest regional the same squad spread out and he reads inside their seventh by 35. The season average lands nine seconds off the scoring group, and 2026 has widened it rather than closed it. It is also the strongest computer science in the metro by both measures (10.4% of 31,855 undergraduates) and, on his residency, the most expensive row here — see the money note below.

The third-closest row on the running is Bethel, and he arrives there as their #1.

What caution means in this metro

Seven of the nine are caution, and at six of them he would be the fastest man on the roster the day he arrived. The two that nearly are not: Bethel, their #1 by 16 seconds at the MIAC championship and by 7 at the Division 3 regional, with seventeen men in that race; and Macalester, their #1 by 17 seconds off a seven that spans 93 seconds and finished third in the conference. Both are places where one runner could pull him along and nobody else could.

At the other end, Hamline and Northwestern–St Paul put seven men on the course and he would lead them by two and a half minutes, and North Central — the closest campus to downtown at 0.9 miles — has never finished five men anywhere except its conference meet: the eight other races on its file, across two seasons, got two, three or four home. Augsburg is the one worth reading twice rather than counting: six finishers spanning twelve minutes, a lone senior at 24:33 who went to the Division 3 national championship by himself, and a 2026 squad whose #1 is now nearly six minutes behind his projection.

The ones worth real effort

  1. St Thomas — 5.5 miles out, D1 Summit League, 4.3% CS of 6,245 undergraduates, $29,155. The only row in the metro where he would not be at or near the front on arrival: 18 seconds outside their seventh man at the conference championship and 14 outside across three fall-2026 races. One honest caveat, and it is on the page rather than buried — their 10K regional reads the other way, where he would have been their #1 by a second, which is what pulls the two-race average to slot 4.5. Division 1, so athletic money exists; ask what share of it the distance group sees.
  2. Minnesota — 1.6 miles, Big Ten, 10.4% CS, and the best computer science in range by a distance. 8th man, 45 seconds outside a Big Ten seven that packs into 47 seconds. Not reachable this year; the thing to measure the rest of the board against, and the one campus here he could reach from downtown on the Green Line.
  3. Macalester and Bethel — 6.7 and 10.3 miles, both MIAC, both Division 3, and the two rows where the caution is 19 and 11 seconds wide across their championships rather than two minutes. Macalester is 7.4% CS and a 29% admit at an $83,864 sticker, with four men who ran a faster 1500 than his projection in May; Bethel is $56,396, 88% admit, and has one man — 3:53.4 for 1500 — who would be ahead of him. Neither is a training group; each is one training partner.

And one for the money rather than the running. Concordia–St Paul is the metro's only Division 2 program, so it is the only place inside the ring where partial athletic aid exists outside the two Division 1 schools, and at $18,462 it is the cheapest row here by $2,282. He would arrive as their #1 by 27 seconds with their seventh man six minutes back, which is the trade this ring keeps offering: the cheaper the row, the thinner the squad.

Six of these nine schools cannot offer him a dollar of athletic money — all six are Division 3, where athletic aid does not exist by rule. The doors that are open are the two Division 1 programs, Minnesota and St Thomas, and Concordia–St Paul in Division 2, where aid is partial by rule. Minnesota is the only public in range, and the residency rule turns it into the most expensive row on the page. Its $16,778 federal net price is computed from in-state students; Minnesota charges non-residents $21,148 more in tuition, so his planning figure is about $37,926 (how that is calculated) — $5,777 dearer than Macalester, which has the metro's highest sticker. The cheapest rows are Concordia–St Paul at $18,462, Hamline at $20,744 and Augsburg at $23,873, and all three come back caution on the running. The metro's median on his residency is $27,705.

No men's cross country at all

One school inside the ring has a real athletics department and no men's team to join: St Catherine University, Division 3, seven driving miles out, with a verified computer science degree — and a women's college. It is listed rather than omitted because the department exists; the institutions inside this ring with no athletics at all — Metropolitan State, Walden and Capella among them — are not rows on any list here.

The 1500 in May

Every school page here carries its 2026 conference-championship 1500 field with his projected 4:01 dropped into it on time, plus that program's own 1500 depth chart for the season. Nine schools share five conference finals — four of them race the MIAC and two the UMAC — so the 1500 measures this metro in five races rather than nine.

His 4:01 clears the last qualifying time in all five of those finals and scores — top eight — in three: 1st of seventeen in the UMAC, 7th of eighteen in the Summit League, 8th of twenty-two in the Northern Sun. The two fields with the real programs in them read the way a healthy gap should: 11th of twenty-eight in the MIAC and 39th of forty-one in the Big Ten — in the race, at the back of it. The Big Ten final is also the one tactical race of the five, won in 4:00.9 off a 3:43.2 prelim, and Minnesota put three men in those prelims and advanced none of them. His 4:01.0 would have been second across the line in that final, which is a fact about how the race was run and not about him — and the clearest warning on this page about reading a 1500 field.

The thinness is in the same numbers. At five of the nine programs he would be the fastest 1500 man on the roster — North Central, Augsburg, Concordia–St Paul, Hamline and Northwestern–St Paul — and the metro's median team best is 4:05.3 against his 4:01.0. Four programs have a man faster than he projects: Minnesota at 3:43.2, Bethel 3:53.4, Macalester 3:55.9 and St Thomas 3:57.1, and that list is the running order of this page from the top. Three of the nine — St Thomas, Concordia–St Paul and Hamline — entered nobody at all in their conference 1500, so those three rows are a comparison against a field their own team did not contest. And none of the nine took a 1500 runner past its conference meet into a postseason round, which is the plainest single measure of the level here. How the 1500 comparison is built.

Three things specific to Minneapolis

The public school is the expensive one, which is backwards from every other metro on this board. Minnesota is the only public inside the ring, and its reported $16,778 net price is an in-state figure; on his residency the planning number is about $37,926, the highest on this page. Every other row is a private charging one rate to everyone, which means the sticker shocks are honest and the discounts are the whole question: the gap between Macalester's $83,864 sticker and its $32,149 average net price is $51,715 of institutional aid, and what share of it he would see is a conversation with a financial aid office rather than a number this board can publish.

Winter is not a detail here, it is the season. The indoor campaign runs through a Twin Cities January, and the difference between a program with a 200m banked track and one with a fieldhouse straightaway is the difference between developing indoors and surviving indoors. Ask each of these nine what they actually train on between December and March, and where the team runs when the river road is ice.

Four of the nine are in Saint Paul, and the whole metro is a day of visits. Macalester, Hamline, Concordia–St Paul and St Thomas sit inside a three-mile square — the St Catherine campus is in the middle of them — and a visit can take in all four plus the three Minneapolis campuses, which are themselves a mile and a half apart, without a long drive. It also shows up in the setting column: the seven city campuses score 73–87 for what he could reach on foot in an ordinary week, while Bethel (29) and Northwestern–St Paul (31) are suburban campuses where a week's errands need a car or a ride. That is a measure of daily life and not of running: the team picks the routes, and no number on this page has an opinion about them.
