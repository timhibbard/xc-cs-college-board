The rule this whole board is built on

Being 40 to 60 seconds behind a team's best 5000 runner is the good outcome. That is a 5th-to-9th man as a freshman: he trains in a pack, gets pulled along, and improves. Arriving as the team's fastest runner is not a sign he is a star — it means the program is thin and there is nobody to train with. A first pass at this analysis had that backwards; the tiers below reflect the corrected framing.

Recommendations this board previously made and has withdrawn

Cross country results contradicted the track data in four places. Hofstra and Loyola Chicago were both recommended as among the best fits in their metros on the strength of a 14:2x outdoor 5000 — but each won its conference championship with a seven he would finish about 80 seconds outside. Both are now cut. Wheaton College, called resolved and upgraded by an earlier version of this page, was the reverse error: at the D3 Midwest regional he would have been their #2, so it dropped to caution. Lewis University was the school an earlier page recommended widening the Chicago radius to reach; its roster data killed it. The pattern is the same every time: a single fastest 5000 is a floor, not a measure of depth. Where the two disagree, the cross country result wins. How that comparison is built.

A fifth withdrawal is about the list rather than a school. This page used to claim it held every college inside each radius that sponsors men's cross country. That was false. Checking the Greenville ring conference by conference found 41 schools that had been inside the original 200 miles all along and never appeared — Southern Wesleyan is 25 miles from home. Sixteen more were missing around New York, ten of them CUNY. Four schools also came off it: Georgia State, NYIT, Montclair State and William Paterson field no men's cross country team, and Trinity Christian is closing.

Those additions entered unmeasured, and measuring them changed the board. They have now been run through the same 2025 championship results as everyone else, and the target band went from 12 schools to 28 — Southern Wesleyan, 25 miles from home, is one of them. Fourteen of the newcomers were unmeasured placeholders a version ago: Southern Wesleyan, Campbell, Chattanooga, Coastal Carolina, College of Charleston, Fayetteville State, Kennesaw State, Mercer, Pfeiffer, Queens, UNC Pembroke, West Georgia, Augusta University and NJIT. Berry College is the fifteenth and it came the other way, up from caution: its track marks looked out of reach and it turns out he would have been their 3rd or 4th man at both the SAA championship and the D3 South regional. The lesson is the one this whole callout keeps making: a tier assigned without a cross country result is a guess, in either direction.

Two schools were cut in error and are back on the board. Clemson and Columbia were both removed on their track marks alone — a 14:36 and a 13:43 5000 read as out of reach. Their actual championship results say otherwise: he would have been Clemson's 8th man at the ACC meet and their 7th at the Southeast regional, and Columbia's #5 at Ivy Heps before slipping to 8th at the Northeast regional. Clemson returns at deep and Columbia at target, 5 miles from midtown. Cutting a school on one fast 5000 is the same mistake as recommending one on it. In the other direction the cross country data confirmed the cuts at Tennessee, Georgia, Georgia Tech, Wake Forest, UNC Chapel Hill and Duke, which is what a working rule looks like: it changes some answers and leaves the rest alone.

And two more schools have no men's team to join. North Georgia sat at Verify here and St. John's sat among the cuts; both sponsor women's cross country only. There was never a men's program at either to verify or to cut.

Three structural findings worth stating plainly

43 of the 129 measured programs never finished seven runners in 2025

This only became visible once every school had a result. A cross country team scores five and the 7th man is the depth number this whole board is built on — and at 43 programs the 7th man does not exist: they never got seven men to the finish of any championship race. Those pages compare him to the team's last finisher instead and say so. Five could not even field the scoring five: Roosevelt, Benedict, John Jay, Mount Saint Vincent and York College (CUNY) finished three or four runners at their conference meet, which is a roster in trouble rather than a team he would join. Being the fastest man on a squad of four is the exact outcome this board exists to avoid, so all five sit at caution no matter how the times read.

Eight schools on these maps have no men's program to join

Chicago is thin on its own merits, not because of the radius

This has now been tested in both directions. An earlier version of this page blamed the 20-mile radius and recommended widening it to 30 to reach Lewis University — "a real distance program, a CS degree, and D2 scholarship money." The radius was widened, Lewis's roster data reversed that conclusion (a team best 5000 of 13:45, a 95 second gap, the same band that cut Furman and Wingate), and the extra ten miles added four other schools and not one target-tier fit. The radius has since come back to 20 miles.

Through all of that, DePaul has been the only target-tier school in Chicago. The tighter ring does cost two things worth naming: North Central, a D3 national-championship program whose 7th man ran within about ten seconds of his projection, and Purdue Northwest, which was the metro's only scholarship program. Roosevelt, one mile from the Loop, was supposed to fill the second of those, and then its GLIAC result came in: four finishers, where five are needed to score. Nothing fills either gap. Measuring the whole metro made the case blunter rather than softer — five of the eleven measured programs here never finished seven, and outside DePaul, UIC and the University of Chicago every school in range puts him at their #1.

What to do next

  1. Start with the 28 target-tier schools, in order of distance. The measurement job is essentially done: 129 of 131 schools have a 2025 championship result behind their tier. Only Calumet College and Shorter are still unmeasured, and both are edge cases rather than oversights — a school marked ◆Verify means unmeasured, not borderline. Shorter entered three men at its only 2025 meet and all three did not start, so there may be nothing to measure.
  2. Email the coaches. Every school page now carries the coach's name, title, office phone and email, read off the school's own staff directory. He can contact any coach in any division at any time; the NCAA restrictions only limit when coaches may initiate. Two schools publish no address (Fayetteville State and West Georgia) — use the phone number or the recruit form there.
  3. Confirm the CS degree at every school marked Confirm — a BA in applied AI is not a BS in computer science.
  4. Ask the thin-squad schools how many men are coming back. A 2025 result showing four or five finishers is one season of evidence, not a verdict; the coach knows whether the roster is rebuilding or collapsing.
  5. Ask about lab scheduling. Required CS labs collide with a 3:00–5:30 practice window. Big schools run multiple sections; small ones may run one.
  6. Model net cost, not scholarship. D1/D2 men's track shares 12.6 scholarship equivalencies across 35–45 athletes. Academic merit aid frequently beats athletic aid outright, and D3 offers no athletic aid at all.
