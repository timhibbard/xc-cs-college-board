The rule this whole board is built on

Being 40 to 60 seconds behind a team's best 5000 runner is the good outcome. That is a 5th-to-9th man as a freshman: he trains in a pack, gets pulled along, and improves. Arriving as the team's fastest runner is not a sign he is a star — it means the program is thin and there is nobody to train with. A first pass at this analysis had that backwards; the tiers below reflect the corrected framing.

Four recommendations this board previously made are now withdrawn

Cross country results arrived for 42 schools, and they contradicted the track data in four places. Hofstra and Loyola Chicago were both recommended as among the best fits in their metros on the strength of a 14:2x outdoor 5000 — but each won its conference championship with a seven he would finish about 80 seconds outside. Both are now cut. Wheaton College, which an earlier version of this page called resolved and upgraded, is the reverse error: at the D3 Midwest regional he would have been their #2, so it drops to caution. And North Central, cut on reputation, is back on the board — their 7th man ran within about ten seconds of his projection.

The pattern is the same every time: a single fastest 5000 is a floor, not a measure of depth. Where the two disagree, the cross country result wins. How that comparison is built.

Two structural findings worth stating plainly

Northwestern has no men's program at all

Chicago is thin on its own merits, not because of the radius

An earlier version of this page blamed the 20-mile radius and recommended widening it to 30 to reach Lewis University — "a real distance program, a CS degree, and D2 scholarship money." The radius has now been widened, and pulling Lewis's roster data reversed that conclusion: their team best 5000 is 13:45, a 95 second gap, which makes Lewis a walk-on program by the same rule that cut Furman and Wingate.

The extra ten miles added four schools and not one target-tier fit. The cross country data then made the metro thinner still: Loyola Chicago is now cut and Wheaton drops to caution, leaving DePaul as the only target-tier school in Chicago, with UIC, the University of Chicago, and North Central sitting just outside their sevens. Three good programs he would not travel for as a freshman is a different problem from a weak metro, but it is still a problem.

What to do next

  1. Get cross country results for the 36 schools that still lack them — almost all of D2 and D3 in the New York metro. Forty-two schools now have their full scoring seven from 2025 championship results, and every one of those lookups changed something. Any school still marked ◆Verify is a tier assigned from one track mark.
  2. Confirm the CS degree at every school marked Confirm — a BA in applied AI is not a BS in computer science.
  3. Email coaches directly. He can contact any coach in any division at any time; the NCAA contact restrictions only limit when coaches may initiate.
  4. Ask about lab scheduling. Required CS labs collide with a 3:00–5:30 practice window. Big schools run multiple sections; small ones may run one.
  5. Model net cost, not scholarship. D1/D2 men's track shares 12.6 scholarship equivalencies across 35–45 athletes. Academic merit aid frequently beats athletic aid outright, and D3 offers no athletic aid at all.
