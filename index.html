The rule this whole board is built on

Being 40 to 60 seconds behind a team's best 5000 runner is the good outcome. That is a 5th-to-9th man as a freshman: he trains in a pack, gets pulled along, and improves. Arriving as the team's fastest runner is not a sign he is a star — it means the program is thin and there is nobody to train with. A first pass at this analysis had that backwards; the tiers below reflect the corrected framing.

Recommendations this board previously made and has withdrawn

Cross country results contradicted the track data in four places. Hofstra and Loyola Chicago were both recommended as among the best fits in their metros on the strength of a 14:2x outdoor 5000 — but each won its conference championship with a seven he would finish about 80 seconds outside. Both are now cut. Wheaton College, called resolved and upgraded by an earlier version of this page, was the reverse error: at the D3 Midwest regional he would have been their #2, so it dropped to caution. Lewis University was the school an earlier page recommended widening the Chicago radius to reach; its roster data killed it. The pattern is the same every time: a single fastest 5000 is a floor, not a measure of depth. Where the two disagree, the cross country result wins. How that comparison is built.

A fifth withdrawal is about the list rather than a school. This page used to claim it held every college inside each radius that sponsors men's cross country. That was false. Checking the Greenville ring conference by conference found 41 schools that had been inside the original 200 miles all along and never appeared — Southern Wesleyan is 25 miles from home. Sixteen more were missing around New York, ten of them CUNY. They are all on the board now, entering at Verify with full cost and admissions data and no running data at all, so none of them changes a ranking. Four schools also came off it: Georgia State, NYIT, Montclair State and William Paterson field no men's cross country team, and Trinity Christian is closing.

Two structural findings worth stating plainly

Six schools on these maps have no men's program to join

Chicago is thin on its own merits, not because of the radius

This has now been tested in both directions. An earlier version of this page blamed the 20-mile radius and recommended widening it to 30 to reach Lewis University — "a real distance program, a CS degree, and D2 scholarship money." The radius was widened, Lewis's roster data reversed that conclusion (a team best 5000 of 13:45, a 95 second gap, the same band that cut Furman and Wingate), and the extra ten miles added four other schools and not one target-tier fit. The radius has since come back to 20 miles.

Through all of that, DePaul has been the only target-tier school in Chicago. The tighter ring does cost two things worth naming: North Central, a D3 national-championship program whose 7th man ran within about ten seconds of his projection, and Purdue Northwest, which was the metro's only scholarship program. Roosevelt, one mile from the Loop, now fills the second of those. Nothing fills the first.

What to do next

  1. Get cross country results for the 93 schools that still lack them. 37 schools have their full scoring seven from 2025 championship results, and every one of those lookups changed something. A school marked ◆Verify is unmeasured, not borderline — the tier is a placeholder for a lookup nobody has done. The highest-value ones: the eleven Greenville-metro schools inside 100 miles that were only just added, Seton Hall's Big East result, and any CUNYAC 8K result at all.
  2. Confirm the CS degree at every school marked Confirm — a BA in applied AI is not a BS in computer science.
  3. Email coaches directly. He can contact any coach in any division at any time; the NCAA contact restrictions only limit when coaches may initiate.
  4. Ask about lab scheduling. Required CS labs collide with a 3:00–5:30 practice window. Big schools run multiple sections; small ones may run one.
  5. Model net cost, not scholarship. D1/D2 men's track shares 12.6 scholarship equivalencies across 35–45 athletes. Academic merit aid frequently beats athletic aid outright, and D3 offers no athletic aid at all.
