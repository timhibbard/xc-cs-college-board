New York City

The ring here is 20 driving miles from Midtown, plus all of Nassau and Suffolk County regardless of distance — a shape, not a circle, because Long Island is a commutable corridor and Westchester and central New Jersey are a different decision. Tightening from 50 miles cost this metro ten schools, including one of its three target-tier fits. Filling in the CUNY system and Long Island added sixteen. Two of the additions reframe the whole metro on cost.

The tighter ring costs Fairfield and Monmouth, and both were real

Fairfield (50 mi, Connecticut) was one of three target-tier schools in this metro: he slotted in as their 6th man at the MAAC championship on a tight 59-second seven. Its NCAA regional 10K disagreed — the same comparison made him their #1 — so it was flagged as the metro's highest-value verification. That question is now moot at 20 miles. Monmouth (45 mi, New Jersey) was Deep tier and unambiguous: the same shape as NYU in the CAA, about 21 seconds outside their seven.

Also out: Army West Point (50 mi — free tuition, excellent CS, real distance program, and a congressional nomination plus a five-year service obligation), Sacred Heart (52), and six Westchester and New Jersey schools — Mercy (25), Pace (30), Manhattanville (30), SUNY Purchase (30), Ramapo (25) and Drew (30). Princeton and Rutgers leave the cut list. None of the eight was tiered above Verify, so the honest accounting is: the radius change cost one Target, one Deep, and eight unknowns.

The finding that changes this metro: ten CUNY colleges

Filling in the CUNY system added eight programs to this board — Hunter, Brooklyn, City College, Lehman, John Jay, York, Medgar Evers and, at Division 2, the College of Staten Island. With Baruch and Queens College, which were already here, that is ten CUNY colleges in range, every one of them sponsoring men's cross country, every one granting a bachelor's in computer science, and every one under $6,000 in federal average net price. The cheapest, Hunter, is $2,984. Hunter and Brooklyn each award about 10% of their bachelor's degrees in computer science, a higher share than Fordham, NYU or any D1 on this page except NJIT. All ten are unmeasured on running.

Read that net price with care, though. It is an average over a student body that is overwhelmingly in-state and living at home. A South Carolina applicant pays the out-of-state rate — about $15,300 at CUNY, still low — and most of these campuses have no dormitories at all, so housing means renting in New York City and that cost appears nowhere in the federal file. The right way to use this: CUNY is the cheapest tuition on the page by a wide margin and the most expensive housing problem on it.

Three schools were on this board in error and have no men's cross country

All three were carrying a Verify tier, which is the failure mode of a tier that means "no data yet": it looks like a program awaiting measurement rather than a program that does not exist. They are kept on the map rather than deleted so the correction is visible.

The ones worth real effort

  1. Fordham — still the best overall fit in the metro, and the only one confirmed on cross country. At the Atlantic 10 championship he would have been their 6th man, 12 seconds inside their scoring seven, with a healthy 95-second 1–7 spread. Add a real CS department and Van Cortlandt Park — the most historic cross country course in America — directly next to campus. It answers the "where do you actually run in New York" problem better than anything else on the list. The catch is money: a $44,338 average net price, the second highest in this metro.
  2. Seton Hall — Big East, and a 14:58 team best that leaves him 20–25 seconds back. It is target tier on a track mark only — no cross country result was found, so treat the ranking as provisional. Their conference championship result is the single most valuable lookup left in this metro.
  3. NJIT — D1, a genuine tech-school CS program at 33% of its degrees, 65% admit, and a $16,504 average net price — less than half of Fordham's and the best D1-plus-CS value in the metro. Still needs roster data of any kind.
  4. Hunter College — the new entry to email first. D3 CUNYAC, two miles from Midtown, men's cross country and track, about 10% of its degrees in CS, and a $2,984 average net price. It is completely unmeasured on running, which is the point: at D3 CUNYAC level the odds that he is inside their seven are good, and it costs one email to find out. Ask for their 2025 CUNYAC 8K results.
  5. Stony Brook — the strongest CS department on Long Island and a full D1 CAA program. It is 60 miles out and only on this board because of the Long Island rule, so treat the commute honestly. Unmeasured on running, and at D1 CAA level that measurement is likely to be unkind — Hofstra, cut below, is the cautionary example from the same conference.
  6. Stevens Institute of Technology — if CS leads the decision. One of the strongest CS and engineering schools on the list, in Hoboken, D3, no athletic-scholarship pressure, 48% admit. Also unverified on running.
Hofstra is cut, and it was one of this page's two headline recommendations

An outdoor 5000 of 14:32 suggested a clean 40-second gap, and this page called Hofstra "clean on both axes." They then won the CAA championship with a top seven of 23:02 to 24:45 — he would have finished roughly 82 seconds outside their scoring seven. That is walk-on territory by the same rule that cut Furman and Iona. The 14:32 was a floor, not a measure of the squad, and the reversal is stated here rather than quietly edited out. Hofstra is in Hempstead, so it stays on the board under the Long Island rule — as a cut.

Wagner drops to Verify for a softer reason: only six runners are on file from the NEC championship, their front two are respectable, and their 4th man ran 28:59 — a 359-second spread across six. Something is wrong with either the squad or the result. Worth one email, not five.

NYU: too good, not too thin

NYU won the UAA championship with seven runners inside 42 seconds; his projection lands about 35 seconds off the back of that. It is now Deep rather than a borderline keep, and with Monmouth out of range it is the metro's only Deep-tier school: a real program where he would train hard and not travel as a freshman. D3 means there is no scholarship at stake in aiming high — but the 9% admit rate is the real gate, and a coach's support is what moves it.

Five schools here sponsor cross country but not men's track

John Jay, Brooklyn College, SUNY Maritime, Sarah Lawrence and SUNY Old Westbury field men's cross country with no men's indoor or outdoor track and field. For someone who wants both seasons that is close to disqualifying, and it is not visible anywhere in the federal data or in a tier — it is noted on each school's own page. Confirm it with the coach before any of them goes on a shortlist; conference sponsorship tables lag reality by a season. SUNY Maritime and the Merchant Marine Academy carry a second constraint: a regiment and summer sea terms, which is a different life, not a detail.

Three things specific to New York

Cost of living is the dominant variable, and the spread is now enormous. Average net price actually paid runs from $2,984 at Hunter to $49,965 at Yeshiva — with Fordham $44,338, Stevens $41,346 and NYU $37,050 at the top, NJIT $16,504 in the middle, and ten CUNY campuses under $6,000. That is a six-figure difference over four years and no partial athletic scholarship in men's distance running comes close to closing it. Two caveats that matter in opposite directions: every figure is an average across all students after grants, not a quote for one applicant; and the cheapest figures belong to commuter campuses with no dormitories, where an out-of-state student's real cost is tuition plus New York rent. Model total cost of attendance, not tuition.

Training terrain is a genuine question to raise with coaches. Ask specifically where the team does long runs and workouts. Good answers: Van Cortlandt Park, Central Park, Prospect Park, the Rockefeller carriage trails, Bear Mountain for hills, Sunken Meadow and Bethpage on Long Island. A bad answer is a hesitation, or "we do a lot on the track." At a commuter school, ask a harder version of the same question: where does the team actually meet, and how many of them get there.

Two states, two sets of in-state tuition, neither of them his. The New Jersey publics (Kean, NJIT, Rutgers–Newark) and the New York publics (the ten CUNYs, Farmingdale, Old Westbury, Stony Brook, Maritime) will all charge out-of-state rates to a South Carolina applicant. The gap is small at CUNY — roughly $7,400 in-state against $15,300 out — and large at Stony Brook, where it is $10,931 against $32,741. Check it school by school before ranking any of them on price.
