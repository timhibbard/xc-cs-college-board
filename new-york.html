New York City

A 50-mile circle around Midtown captures more colleges than the other two metros combined, spanning three states. The athletic fits are good and several are genuinely underrated. The variable that will actually decide this metro is not running or admissions — it is cost of living.

The ones worth real effort

  1. Fordham — the best overall fit in the metro. Atlantic 10 D1, a real CS department, a clean 25–35 second gap to their #1, and they train at Van Cortlandt Park, the most historic cross country course in America, directly next to campus. It answers the "where do you actually run in New York" problem better than anyone else on the list.
  2. Hofstra — Coastal Athletic D1, roughly 40 seconds back, a Long Island campus with actual space to train, and ~70% acceptance. Clean on both axes.
  3. Wagner — Staten Island, ~40 second gap, easy admission, D1. Underrated.
  4. Seton Hall — Big East, 25–35 seconds back. Their distance squad is weaker than the conference name suggests, which works in his favor.
  5. Stevens Institute of Technology — if CS leads the decision. One of the strongest CS and engineering schools on the list, in Hoboken, D3, with no athletic-scholarship pressure. Get their roster times before investing time here.
  6. NJIT — D1 with a genuine tech-school CS program at ~65% acceptance. The best D1-plus-CS value in the metro if the running fits. Needs roster data.
Borderline keep: NYU

A 13:56 team best and a 29:45 10K make this a top-tier D3 program, leaving him 75–80 seconds off their #1. He likely would not travel as a freshman. It stayed on the list because D3 has no scholarship stakes, so the downside of aiming high is lower — but verify depth before investing.

Three things specific to New York

Cost of living is the dominant variable. Fordham, NYU, and Stevens are among the most expensive schools in the country once housing is counted, and NYC-area housing will swamp a partial athletic scholarship. The public options — Montclair State, Kean, William Paterson, Ramapo, the CUNYs, Queens College — change the math entirely. Model total cost of attendance including housing, not tuition.

Training terrain is a genuine question to raise with coaches. Ask specifically where the team does long runs and workouts. Good answers: Van Cortlandt Park, Central Park, the Rockefeller State Park carriage trails, Bear Mountain for hills, the Long Island parks. A bad answer is a hesitation, or "we do a lot on the track."

Three states means three sets of in-state tuition. The New Jersey publics (Montclair, Kean, Ramapo, William Paterson, NJIT) and the New York publics (the CUNYs, Queens College, Purchase, Farmingdale) will all charge out-of-state rates to a South Carolina applicant. Factor that in before ranking any of them on price.

Army West Point deserves a separate note: free tuition, excellent CS, and a real distance program — but it requires a congressional nomination and carries a five-year active-duty service obligation. That is a different life decision, not just a college choice.
