New York City

A 50-mile circle around Midtown captures more colleges than the other two metros combined, spanning three states. It is also the metro the cross country data hit hardest: Hofstra, previously recommended here as one of the two best fits, is now cut, and only three schools are left in the target tier. Twenty-four of the thirty-four still have no cross country results at all, which is where the remaining work in this metro is. The variable that will decide it, though, is cost of living.

The ones worth real effort

  1. Fordham — still the best overall fit in the metro, and now the only one confirmed on cross country. At the Atlantic 10 championship he would have been their 6th man, 12 seconds inside their scoring seven, with a healthy 95-second 1–7 spread. Add a real CS department and Van Cortlandt Park — the most historic cross country course in America — directly next to campus. It answers the "where do you actually run in New York" problem better than anything else on the list. The catch is money: a $44,338 average net price, the second highest in this metro.
  2. Fairfield — the strongest new find here, and the one to verify first. He slots in as their 6th man at the MAAC championship on a tight 59-second seven, which is exactly the target pattern. But their NCAA regional 10K tells a different story, where the same comparison makes him their #1. Two races, two answers three or more places apart. Also: the federal admit rate is 33%, not the ~57% this site previously estimated, so it is a genuinely selective school.
  3. Seton Hall — Big East, and a 14:58 team best that leaves him 20–25 seconds back. It is target tier on a track mark only — no cross country result was found, so treat the ranking as provisional. Their conference championship result is the single most valuable lookup left in this metro.
  4. NJIT — D1, a genuine tech-school CS program, 65% admit, and a $16,504 average net price — less than half of Fordham's and the best D1-plus-CS value in the metro by a wide margin. Still needs roster data of any kind.
  5. Stevens Institute of Technology — if CS leads the decision. One of the strongest CS and engineering schools on the list, in Hoboken, D3, no athletic-scholarship pressure, 48% admit. Also unverified on running.
Hofstra is cut, and it was one of this page's two headline recommendations

An outdoor 5000 of 14:32 suggested a clean 40-second gap, and this page called Hofstra "clean on both axes." They then won the CAA championship with a top seven of 23:02 to 24:45 — he would have finished roughly 82 seconds outside their scoring seven. That is walk-on territory by the same rule that cut Furman, Iona and Lewis. The 14:32 was a floor, not a measure of the squad, and the reversal is stated here rather than quietly edited out.

Wagner drops to Verify for a softer reason: only six runners are on file from the NEC championship, their front two are respectable, and their 4th man ran 28:59 — a 359-second spread across six. Something is wrong with either the squad or the result. Worth one email, not five.

NYU and Monmouth: too good, not too thin

NYU won the UAA championship with seven runners inside 42 seconds; his projection lands about 35 seconds off the back of that. Monmouth is the same shape in the CAA, about 21 seconds outside their seven. Both are now Deep rather than borderline keeps: real programs where he would train hard and not travel as a freshman. At NYU, D3 means there is no scholarship at stake in aiming high — but the 9% admit rate is the real gate, and a coach's support is what moves it.

Three things specific to New York

Cost of living is the dominant variable, and the federal numbers now quantify it. Average net price actually paid: Fairfield $48,095, Fordham $44,338, Stevens $41,346, NYU $37,050 — against NJIT $16,504 and the CUNYs and New Jersey publics lower still. That is a $25,000-a-year spread, or a six-figure difference over four years, and no partial athletic scholarship in men's distance running closes it. Every figure is an average across all students after grants and aid, not a quote for one applicant, but the ordering is real. Model total cost of attendance, not tuition.

Training terrain is a genuine question to raise with coaches. Ask specifically where the team does long runs and workouts. Good answers: Van Cortlandt Park, Central Park, the Rockefeller State Park carriage trails, Bear Mountain for hills, the Long Island parks. A bad answer is a hesitation, or "we do a lot on the track."

Three states means three sets of in-state tuition. The New Jersey publics (Montclair, Kean, Ramapo, William Paterson, NJIT) and the New York publics (the CUNYs, Queens College, Purchase, Farmingdale) will all charge out-of-state rates to a South Carolina applicant. Factor that in before ranking any of them on price.

Army West Point deserves a separate note: free tuition, excellent CS, and a real distance program — but it requires a congressional nomination and carries a five-year active-duty service obligation. That is a different life decision, not just a college choice.
