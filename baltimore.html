UMBC is the best computer-science-plus-running row in this pass

26.8% computer science, Division 1 in America East, 72% admit, $34,486 on his residency as a Maryland public, and on the running he is their 6th man, 41 seconds inside their seven and 68 behind their #1. There is no other row on this board that pairs a CS share above 25% with a target-tier measurement and an admit rate above 20%.

The one caveat is the price basis: UMBC is a public, so the federal net price is an in-state figure and the number above adds the non-resident premium the way this board does everywhere. A non-resident waiver or an athletic share would change it materially, and Division 1 means both exist in principle.

Johns Hopkins, and the two cheap MEAC publics

Johns Hopkins is deep by four seconds — their seventh man is that close — with 10.5% CS, a $18,809 net price and a 6% admit rate. Division 3, so no athletic money; it is an admissions conversation like Harvard and MIT.

Coppin State at $16,901 and Morgan State at $25,880 are the two Division 1 MEAC publics, 3.5 and 4.6 miles out, and both are thin: Coppin State never finished seven runners, and at Morgan State he would arrive level with their #1 and 367 seconds inside their seventh. The money and the location are real; ask both how many men are signed for the fall.

The ones worth real effort

  1. UMBC — D1 America East, 8 miles, 26.8% CS, 72% admit, $34,486 on his residency, and his 6th man slot is 41 seconds inside their seven. Best row on the page by a distance, and the residency question is the first thing to ask the coach.
  2. Johns Hopkins — D3 Centennial, 3.6 miles, 10.5% CS, $18,809, 6% admit, and four seconds outside their seventh man. A real training environment where he would not score early, behind a hard admissions door.
  3. Morgan State — D1 MEAC, 4.6 miles, 8% CS, 82% admit, $25,880. Caution tier because he would arrive as their #2 and level with their #1, which is a thin squad rather than a bad school; Division 1 money and an open door make it worth the email anyway.

And the row that is not on the list. It is on the no-track table below because its athletics site publishes a combined cross country section and women's track and field only. Goucher and Stevenson round out the page at caution: 14.2 and 18.8 miles, $22,470 and $26,505, and he would lead both.

Three of these six are Division 1 and three are Division 3, so there is no Division 2 partial-aid door in range at all. The Division 1 three — UMBC, Coppin State and Morgan State — are all Maryland publics, which means all three carry a non-resident premium on top of the federal net price and all three can in principle waive it. The metro median is $24,175, the cheapest rows are Coppin State at $16,901 and Johns Hopkins at $18,809, and the most expensive is UMBC at $34,486 — which is the same row as the best fit, so the residency conversation matters more here than anywhere else in this pass.
