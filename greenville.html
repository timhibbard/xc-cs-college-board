The five that matter

  1. UNC Asheville — 62 miles, Big South, mountain terrain, roughly 40–50s behind their #1, and an acceptance rate near 90%. The best combination of proximity, athletic fit, and admissions ease on the entire board.
  2. Davidson — his times land inside their top 8 and coach support is genuine admissions leverage at ~18% acceptance. This is the highest-value application on the list: running unlocks a school his transcript alone might not.
  3. ETSU — Southern Conference, real distance history, a clean 45–55 second gap. Straightforward fit.
  4. Emory — if academics lead the decision. Elite CS, D3 UAA, and he would be top-2 immediately. No athletic money at D3, but strong need-based aid.
  5. Catawba, Lenoir-Rhyne, Anderson (SC) — the D2 tier is where actual scholarship dollars live, and all three have a healthy gap to their #1.
Two boundary cases the data cannot settle

Appalachian State (team best 14:01) and UNC Charlotte (14:10) leave him 70–85 seconds off their fastest runner. That gap is normal for a recruited freshman measured against a senior, so both stayed on the list — but they sit right on the line, and a 7th-man time would decide it either way.

The sharpest trade-off on the board: Wofford

Strong academics, a verified CS degree, 32 miles from home — and a team best 5000 of 15:27 that he would beat the day he arrived. Being a team's #1 is workable if the coach is building a recruiting class around him. Ask directly who else they are signing. It is bad if he trains alone for four years.

Money and admissions

Recruitment is an admissions lever at the selective schools. Coaches at Davidson and Emory get supported slots and will run a pre-read: send transcript and scores, and admissions tells the coach informally whether he would be admitted. Initiate this in the spring of junior year.

Where academics are an asset rather than a constraint: UNC Asheville (~90%), UNC Greensboro (~88%), ETSU (~85%), Western Carolina (~85%), and nearly the whole D2 list. Admission is close to automatic, which shifts all the leverage to money.

Academic aid often beats athletic aid. D1 cross country and track is a 12.6-scholarship equivalency spread across a 35–45 man roster — quarter and half rides, not full ones. A strong student stacking merit aid at UNC Asheville, ETSU, High Point, Berry, or Catawba can land below the net cost of a bigger athletic offer somewhere else.

CS labs collide with practice. Large schools like Charlotte and App State run multiple lab sections; small ones — Wofford, Presbyterian, Berry, Piedmont, North Greenville, Catawba — may offer a required course in exactly one afternoon slot. Ask every coach how many of their athletes major in CS or engineering and how they handle lab conflicts. This question eliminates programs faster than times do.
