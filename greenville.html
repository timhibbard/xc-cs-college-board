Greenville, South Carolina

The deepest and best list of the three metros, and the one the cross country data improved: nine schools here have him finishing inside their scoring seven, more than the other two metros combined. It also lost the most to the walk-on cut, because the two closest schools with the best CS departments are both nationally elite distance programs. Widening the ring from 200 to 250 miles more than tripled this list — from 27 schools to 81 — but almost none of that growth came from the extra fifty miles. It came from admitting that the original list was incomplete.

The real correction here is not the extra fifty miles — it is that this list was incomplete

Checking the ring properly, conference by conference, turned up roughly 40 schools that were inside the original 200 miles all along and had simply never been found: most of Conference Carolinas, most of the SIAC and the CIAA, the Peach Belt, the USA South, half the South Atlantic Conference. Southern Wesleyan is 25 driving miles away and was not on this page. Adding a school at 230 miles while omitting one at 25 is not defensible, so the fix was to add all of them: every NCAA Division 1, 2 and 3 school inside 250 driving miles that sponsors men's cross country, plus one non-NCAA program close to home (Montreat, NAIA, at 70 miles).

That takes the list from 27 schools to 81, and the README's earlier claim that this board listed every school in range was false when it was written. It is closer to true now. Two known gaps remain, and both are named in the methodology: the NAIA and NCCAA programs in this footprint are not systematically covered, and sponsorship was read off conference tables, so a school whose conference table is stale can still be wrong in either direction.

What it does not change: the ranking. All 55 additions enter at Verify with full federal cost, admissions and CS data and no cross country data at all. Verify here means unmeasured, not borderline. The nine target-tier schools, the two Deep and the thirteen Caution are exactly the same schools they were before.

Georgia State was on this board in error — there is no men's program

It sat at Verify tier, which is the failure mode of a tier that means "no data yet": it looked like a program awaiting measurement rather than one that does not exist. It is kept on the map, greyed, rather than deleted, so the correction stays visible.

The five that matter

Every ranking below is anchored to a 2025 cross country result rather than to one outdoor 5000 mark, so each one names where he would actually have finished in that team's seven. None of the 55 schools added to this page has such a result yet, so none of them appears here — the top picks are unchanged by the radius change, and will stay that way until somebody looks up a conference championship.

  1. UNC Asheville — 62 miles, Big South, mountain terrain, a $12,250 average net price and a 92% admit rate. He slots in as their 6th man at the Big South and 7th at the regional, 50 seconds inside their scoring seven. The best combination of proximity, athletic fit, admissions ease, and price on the entire board.
  2. Davidson — their 4th man, and coach support is real admissions leverage at a 13% admit rate. The highest-value application on the list: running unlocks a school his transcript alone might not. The caveat is their own shape — a big gap between their #1 and their 4th through 7th, so he would be filling a hole rather than joining a pack.
  3. UNC Charlotte — the boundary case the data resolved, and it resolved upward. He lands essentially level with their 7th man (11 seconds inside) on a soft 167-second 1–7 spread. 100 miles, D1 American, 80% admit, $15,435 net, and one of the larger CS departments on the board.
  4. Emory — the biggest single correction in this metro. Its 15:06 outdoor 5000 was a sparse-table artifact; on the UAA result he is their 5th man, not their #1. Elite CS, D3, no athletic money but strong need-based aid, and an 11% admit rate that a coach's support genuinely moves.
  5. ETSU and UNC Greensboro — both put him around their 4th or 5th man in the Southern Conference, both admit at 86–89%, and UNC Greensboro is the cheapest target-tier school on the board at $10,965 net. Read ETSU's 171-second spread as a warning though: a team that strung out that badly is not a pack.
The two boundary cases are settled, and they split

UNC Charlotte moves up. Their seven at the American championship ran within 11 seconds of his projection at the back, so he is in the travel squad as a freshman. That is a target.

Appalachian State moves down to Deep. Their 7th man is about 35 seconds faster than his projection — a strong Sun Belt program he would train with but not travel for in year one. Not a cut, and not a target either: worth an email that asks specifically what their incoming distance class looks like.

Anderson (SC) also drops to Deep, and it is the most carefully checked school on the board. At 30 miles it was the closest target-tier school here, so it got the most scrutiny: their South Atlantic Conference course ran about 96 seconds slow at 8K (Anderson, Catawba and Wingate each confirm that against the D2 regional), and once corrected he sits within two seconds of their 7th man — the closest call anywhere in this project. Two seconds is not a distinction; treat Anderson as a real option and ask the coach.

Lenoir-Rhyne is withdrawn from the top picks

An earlier version of this page grouped Lenoir-Rhyne with Catawba and Anderson as a healthy D2 fit. Their cross country result does not support it: their scoring six spanned 326 seconds at the D2 Southeast regional, and he would arrive as their #1. That is the thin-squad pattern this whole board is built to avoid. Catawba survives the same test — 5th man, 95 seconds inside their seven — so the D2 scholarship argument is still live, just at Catawba and Anderson rather than at Lenoir-Rhyne.

The sharpest trade-off on the board: Wofford

Strong academics, a verified CS degree, 32 miles from home — and a team best 5000 of 15:27 that he would beat the day he arrived. Being a team's #1 is workable if the coach is building a recruiting class around him. Ask directly who else they are signing. It is bad if he trains alone for four years.

Cut as walk-on

Worth naming the cost plainly: this removes Furman (0 miles) and Clemson (32 miles) — the two closest schools with the strongest CS departments in the region. If a walk-on or redshirt path ever becomes acceptable, they return at the top of the list, not the bottom.

Four rows on this table used to read "out of range," which was simply wrong — Columbia SC is 105 driving miles from Greenville, Athens 140, Knoxville 145 and Winston-Salem 175, all inside even the old 200-mile ring. South Carolina, Georgia, Tennessee and Wake Forest now carry the real reason, which is level rather than distance. UNC Chapel Hill and Duke join them, having genuinely come into range at 250 miles. All six are cuts by reputation, not by measurement: no 2025 times have been transcribed for any of them, and each row says so.

How big the school is, and how big the town is

Two new columns in the table above, and they only appear on this page: Students (undergraduate enrollment, federal) and Town (the population of the place the campus actually sits in, U.S. Census ACS 2024 5-year). New York and Chicago do not need them. Around Greenville they matter, because the 250-mile ring reaches from Charlotte to towns of six hundred people, and nothing else in the table tells those two apart.

The median school here has 1,867 undergraduates in a town of 32,078, but the median hides the shape of it: 26 of the 81 sit in a place under 10,000 people and 24 sit in one over 100,000. There is not much in between.

Five campuses have more undergraduates than their town has residents — North Georgia (16,146 students, Dahlonega's 7,299), UNC Pembroke (5,330 / 2,822), Western Carolina (10,024 / 7,973), Kennesaw State (41,254 / 34,605) and Montreat (762 / 630). App State is near-parity at 19,444 in a Boone of 20,032. In all six the school is the town, which is a real answer to "what is there to do" and not automatically a bad one for someone whose afternoons are spoken for.

At the other end are the genuinely small places: Montreat (630), Misenheimer NC (659 — and Pfeiffer enrolls 655, so school and town are the same size), Franklin Springs GA (1,108), Due West SC (1,177) and Young Harris GA (1,252). Ask those coaches exactly one question: where does the team run. A 12-mile long run out of a town of six hundred is either trails and gravel, which is ideal, or a state highway with no shoulder, which is not.

Size cuts both ways on the two things that actually decide this. A small college means a small training group — fewer people to chase, which is the whole point of the framing rule — and it means the CS lab-conflict problem below is at its worst. A big state school means depth to chase and multiple lab sections, and it means he is one of forty distance runners competing for a coach's attention rather than one of eight. The target tier spans the entire range: Catawba, 1,235 students in Salisbury, and UNC Charlotte, 24,453 in a city of 903,844, are the same tier and completely different lives.

Neither column moves any tier, and both carry caveats worth knowing before quoting them — Augusta, Macon and Athens are consolidated city-county governments so their numbers are county-wide, four of these places are census-designated rather than incorporated, and a small figure inside Atlanta (Morrow GA, 6,364) describes the address rather than the setting. Read the town column next to the Mi column, never alone. The sources and all four caveats are in §6.

Money and admissions

Recruitment is an admissions lever at the selective schools. Coaches at Davidson and Emory get supported slots and will run a pre-read: send transcript and scores, and admissions tells the coach informally whether he would be admitted. Initiate this in the spring of junior year. The federal numbers make the case sharper than the earlier estimates did: Davidson admits 13% and Emory 11%, and he would be inside the scoring seven at both.

Where academics are an asset rather than a constraint: UNC Asheville (92%), Appalachian State (90%), UNC Greensboro (89%), ETSU (86%), and nearly the whole D2 list — all figures now from federal data rather than estimates. Admission is close to automatic, which shifts all the leverage to money. Two estimates on this page were badly wrong before that check: Wofford is 52%, not ~70%, and Lander is 81%, not ~55%.

Academic aid often beats athletic aid. D1 cross country and track is a 12.6-scholarship equivalency spread across a 35–45 man roster — quarter and half rides, not full ones. A strong student stacking merit aid at UNC Asheville, ETSU, High Point, Berry, or Catawba can land below the net cost of a bigger athletic offer somewhere else.

The wider ring added nine in-state options, which is the cheapest lever available. A South Carolina resident pays in-state tuition at USC Aiken ($10,760), SC State ($11,060), Francis Marion ($11,160), Coastal Carolina ($11,640), The Citadel ($12,570) and the College of Charleston ($12,978), alongside Lander, USC Upstate and Winthrop, which were already here. Those are sticker tuition figures, not net, and they are lower than the net price of most of the private colleges on this page. It also added 35 new Division 2 programs, which is where partial athletic aid actually lives: D2 allows 12.6 scholarship equivalencies in men's cross country and track, the same as D1, on much smaller rosters.

CS labs collide with practice. Large schools like Charlotte and App State run multiple lab sections; small ones — Wofford, Presbyterian, Berry, Piedmont, North Greenville, Catawba, and nearly every one of the small colleges just added — may offer a required course in exactly one afternoon slot. Ask every coach how many of their athletes major in CS or engineering and how they handle lab conflicts. This question eliminates programs faster than times do.
